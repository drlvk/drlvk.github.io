  1. One hundred people were surveyed, and one question was about their educational background. The results, together with their genders, are given in the table below. If a person is selected at random from those surveyed, find the probability of each of the following events.

    Female \(F\)Male \(\overline{F}\)Total
    College degree \(D\)232750
    No college degree \(\overline{D}\)153550
    Total3862100
    1. The person is female or has a college degree.
    2. The person is male or does not have a college degree.
    3. The person is female or does not have a college degree.
    Answer

    (a) \(0.65\)    (b) \(0.77\)    (c) \(0.73\)

    Solution

    Use the addition rule \(P(A\text{ or }B)\) \(=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\text{ and }B)\); the last term keeps the people who belong to both groups from being counted twice.

    (a) \(P(F\text{ or }D)\) \(=\dfrac{38}{100}+\dfrac{50}{100}-\dfrac{23}{100}\) \(=\dfrac{65}{100}\) \(=0.65\). Check: the only people who are neither female nor college graduates are the 35 men without a degree, and \(1-\tfrac{35}{100}=0.65\).

    (b) \(P(\overline{F}\text{ or }\overline{D})\) \(=\dfrac{62+50-35}{100}\) \(=\dfrac{77}{100}\) \(=0.77\). This is everyone except the 23 women with a degree.

    (c) \(P(F\text{ or }\overline{D})\) \(=\dfrac{38+50-15}{100}\) \(=\dfrac{73}{100}\) \(=0.73\). This is everyone except the 27 men with a degree.

  2. If \(A\) and \(B\) are mutually exclusive events with \(P(A)=0.4\) and \(P(B)=0.5\), find the following probabilities.

    1. \(P(A\text{ and }B)\)
    2. \(P(A\text{ or }B)\)
    3. \(P(\overline{A})\)
    4. \(P(\overline{B})\)
    5. \(P(\overline{A\text{ or }B})\)
    6. \(P(A\text{ and }\overline{B})\)
    Answer

    (a) \(0\)    (b) \(0.9\)    (c) \(0.6\)    (d) \(0.5\)    (e) \(0.1\)    (f) \(0.4\)

    Solution

    (a) Mutually exclusive events cannot happen together, so \(P(A\text{ and }B)=0\).

    (b) By the addition rule, \(P(A\text{ or }B)\) \(=0.4+0.5-0\) \(=0.9\).

    (c), (d) By the complement rule, \(P(\overline{A})\) \(=1-0.4\) \(=0.6\) and \(P(\overline{B})\) \(=1-0.5\) \(=0.5\).

    (e) This is the complement of the event in (b): \(1-0.9=0.1\).

    (f) Whenever \(A\) happens, \(B\) does not, so \(P(A\text{ and }\overline{B})\) \(=P(A)-P(A\text{ and }B)\) \(=0.4-0\) \(=0.4\).

  4. In a survey of 258 people, the following data were obtained relating gender to political orientation. A person is randomly selected. Find the probability that the person is:

    Republican (R)Democrat (D)Independent (I)Total
    Male (M)883011129
    Female (F)813612129
    Total1696623258
    1. Male.
    2. Male and a Democrat.
    3. Male, given that the person is a Democrat.
    4. Republican, given that the person is male.
    5. Female, given that the person is an Independent.
    6. Are the events “Female” and “Independent” independent? Answer yes or no.
    Answer

    (a) \(0.5\)    (b) \(\tfrac{30}{258}=\tfrac{5}{43}\approx 0.1163\)    (c) \(\tfrac{30}{66}=\tfrac{5}{11}\approx 0.4545\)    (d) \(\tfrac{88}{129}\approx 0.6822\)    (e) \(\tfrac{12}{23}\approx 0.5217\)    (f) no

    Solution

    (a) \(P(M)\) \(=\tfrac{129}{258}\) \(=0.5\).

    (b) There are 30 male Democrats out of 258 people: \(\tfrac{30}{258}\) \(\approx 0.1163\).

    (c) For a conditional probability, restrict to the given group. Among the 66 Democrats, 30 are male: \(P(M\mid D)\) \(=\tfrac{30}{66}\) \(\approx 0.4545\).

    (d) Among the 129 males, 88 are Republicans: \(P(R\mid M)\) \(=\tfrac{88}{129}\) \(\approx 0.6822\).

    (e) Among the 23 Independents, 12 are female: \(P(F\mid I)\) \(=\tfrac{12}{23}\) \(\approx 0.5217\).

    (f) The events would be independent if \(P(F\mid I)=P(F)\). But \(P(F)\) \(=\tfrac{129}{258}\) \(=0.5\), while \(P(F\mid I)\approx 0.5217\). These differ, so the events are not independent.

    3839424549545861656870
    71757679848589919293162
    1. Find the five-number summary: the minimum, \(Q_1\), \(Q_2\), \(Q_3\), and the maximum.
    2. Find the interquartile range IQR, \(Q_3+1.5\,\text{IQR}\), and \(Q_1-1.5\,\text{IQR}\). Give your answers to two decimal places.
    3. Are there any outliers in this data set?
    Answer

    (a) Min \(=38\), \(Q_1=54\), \(Q_2=70.5\), \(Q_3=85\), Max \(=162\)
    (b) \(\text{IQR}=31.00\), \(Q_3+1.5\,\text{IQR}=131.50\), \(Q_1-1.5\,\text{IQR}=7.50\)

    Solution

    There are \(n=22\) values. To find the \(k\)th percentile, compute the locator \(L=\tfrac{k}{100}\cdot n\). If \(L\) is not a whole number, round it up and take that value; if it is a whole number, average the \(L\)th and \((L+1)\)st values.

    (a) \(Q_2\) is the median: the average of the 11th and 12th values, \(\tfrac{70+71}{2}=70.5\). For \(Q_1\), the locator is \(L=0.25\cdot 22=5.5\), so take the 6th value, \(54\). For \(Q_3\), the locator is \(L=0.75\cdot 22=16.5\), so take the 17th value, \(85\).

    (b) \(\text{IQR}=85-54=31\), so \(1.5\,\text{IQR}=46.5\). Then \(Q_3+1.5\,\text{IQR}\) \(=131.5\) and \(Q_1-1.5\,\text{IQR}=7.5\).

    (c) Values above 131.5 or below 7.5 are outliers. The maximum, 162, is above 131.5; nothing is below 7.5.

  12. This problem has two independent parts.

    Part A. Standard automobile license plates in a country (using the English alphabet) display 5 digits followed by 4 letters.

    1. How many different plates are possible if repetition of letters and digits is allowed?
    2. How many different plates are possible if repetition is not allowed?

    Part B. A middle school class has 12 students.

    1. In how many ways can all 12 students be arranged in a row for a photo?
    2. How many different groups of 6 students can be formed?
    3. The teacher selects 4 students: the first will be Media Helper, the second Pledge/Flag Helper, the third Pencil Sharpener, and the fourth Attendance Taker. In how many ways can this be done?
    Answer

    (a) \(45{,}697{,}600{,}000\)    (b) \(10{,}850{,}112{,}000\)    (c) \(479{,}001{,}600\)    (d) \(924\)    (e) \(11{,}880\)

    Solution

    (a) By the multiplication rule, each of the 5 digit positions has 10 choices and each of the 4 letter positions has 26: \(10^5\cdot 26^4\) \(=45{,}697{,}600{,}000\).

    (b) Without repetition, each position has one fewer choice than the one before: \((10\cdot 9\cdot 8\cdot 7\cdot 6)(26\cdot 25\cdot 24\cdot 23)\) \(=30{,}240\cdot 358{,}800\) \(=10{,}850{,}112{,}000\).

    (c) Arranging all 12 students in order: \(12!=479{,}001{,}600\).

    (d) A group has no order, so count combinations: \({}_{12}C_6\) \(=\dfrac{12!}{6!\,6!}\) \(=924\).

    (e) The four jobs are different, so order matters; count permutations: \({}_{12}P_4\) \(=12\cdot 11\cdot 10\cdot 9\) \(=11{,}880\).

  14. A test consists of 5 multiple-choice questions, each with 5 possible answers. An unprepared student guesses at random on every question. Give each answer as a fraction or rounded to 5 decimal places.

  15. A bag contains 5 red, 6 white, 9 yellow, and 10 blue marbles. Two marbles are selected at random, one after the other. Give each answer as a fraction or rounded to 4 decimal places.

    1. The selection is done without replacement. Find the probability that both marbles are blue.
    2. The selection is done without replacement. Find the probability that the first marble is yellow and the second is blue.
    3. The selection is done with replacement. Find the probability that both marbles are yellow.
    4. The selection is done with replacement. Find the probability that the first marble is white and the second is red.
    Answer

    (a) \(\tfrac{3}{29}\approx 0.1034\)    (b) \(\tfrac{3}{29}\approx 0.1034\)    (c) \(0.09\)    (d) \(\tfrac{1}{30}\approx 0.0333\)

    Solution

    There are \(5+6+9+10=30\) marbles. Without replacement, the second draw is from the 29 marbles that are left.

    (a) \(\dfrac{10}{30}\cdot\dfrac{9}{29}\) \(=\dfrac{90}{870}\) \(=\dfrac{3}{29}\) \(\approx 0.1034\).

    (b) \(\dfrac{9}{30}\cdot\dfrac{10}{29}\) \(=\dfrac{90}{870}\) \(=\dfrac{3}{29}\) \(\approx 0.1034\). (All 10 blue marbles are still in the bag for the second draw.)

    (c) With replacement, the draws are independent: \(\left(\dfrac{9}{30}\right)^2\) \(=0.3^2\) \(=0.09\).

    (d) \(\dfrac{6}{30}\cdot\dfrac{5}{30}\) \(=\dfrac{30}{900}\) \(=\dfrac{1}{30}\) \(\approx 0.0333\).

  16. In a large population, 60% of workers are employed full time. If 10 workers are randomly selected from this population, find the probability of each event. Round your answers to 3 decimal places.

    1. At least 5 of them are employed full time.
    2. None of them is employed full time.
    3. At least 3 of them are employed full time.
    4. At most 4 of them are employed full time.
    Answer

    (a) \(0.834\)    (b) \(0.000\) (since \(0.4^{10}\approx 0.0001049\))    (c) \(0.988\)    (d) \(0.166\)

    Solution

    The number \(X\) of full-time workers is binomial with \(n=10\) and \(p=0.6\), so \(q=0.4\) and \(P(X\) \(=k)\) \(={}_{10}C_k\,(0.6)^k(0.4)^{10-k}\).

    (a) \(P(X\ge 5)\) \(=1-P(X\le 4)\) \(\approx 1-0.1662\) \(=0.8338\), using a calculator (for example, \(1-\text{binomcdf}(10,0.6,4)\)) or a binomial table.

    (b) \(P(X\) \(=0)\) \(=(0.4)^{10}\) \(\approx 0.0001049\), which rounds to \(0.000\).

    (c) Use the complement:

    \[P(X\ge 3)=1-[P(0)+P(1)+P(2)]\] \[\approx 1-(0.000105+0.001573+0.010617)\approx 0.9877.\]

    (d) This is the complement of (a): \(P(X\le 4)\) \(=1-P(X\ge 5)\) \(\approx 0.1662\).

  18. Assume that \(X\) is a binomial random variable with \(n=66\) and \(p=0.27\).

    1. Find the mean \(\mu\) (2 decimal places), the variance \(\sigma^2\) (4 decimal places), and the standard deviation \(\sigma\) (4 decimal places).
    2. Use a calculator or software to find \(P(X\gt 38)\) and \(P(X\lt 33)\), rounded to 4 decimal places.
    Answer

    (a) \(\mu=17.82\), \(\sigma^2=13.0086\), \(\sigma\approx 3.6067\)
    (b) \(P(X\gt 38)\approx 0.0000\) (about \(4.4\times 10^{-8}\)); \(P(X\lt 33)\approx 0.9999\)

    Solution

    (a) For a binomial distribution, \(\mu=np=66(0.27)=17.82\), \(\sigma^2\) \(=npq\) \(=66(0.27)(0.73)\) \(=13.0086\), and \(\sigma\) \(=\sqrt{13.0086}\) \(\approx 3.6067\).

    (b) \(X\) takes whole-number values, so \(P(X\gt 38)=1-P(X\le 38)\), which is \(1-\text{binomcdf}(66,0.27,38)\) \(\approx 4.4\times 10^{-8}\), which rounds to \(0.0000\): 38 is about 5.6 standard deviations above the mean. Similarly, \(P(X\lt 33)\) \(=P(X\le 32)\) \(=\text{binomcdf}(66,0.27,32)\) \(\approx 0.99994\), which rounds to \(0.9999\).

  19. Three-letter “words” are formed using the letters A, B, C, D, E, F, G. How many such words are possible under each of the following conditions? (Letters may be repeated unless a part says otherwise.)

    1. No condition is imposed.
    2. No letter can be repeated in a word.
    3. Each word must begin with the letter A.
    4. The letter C must be at the end.
    5. The second letter must be a vowel.
    Answer

    (a) 343    (b) 210    (c) 49    (d) 49    (e) 98

    Solution

    Use the multiplication rule, one position at a time.

    (a) \(7\cdot 7\cdot 7=343\).   (b) \(7\cdot 6\cdot 5=210\).

    (c) The first letter must be A (1 choice): \(1\cdot 7\cdot 7=49\).   (d) The last letter must be C: \(7\cdot 7\cdot 1=49\).

    (e) The vowels among A–G are A and E, so the middle position has 2 choices: \(7\cdot 2\cdot 7=98\).

  31. The weights of unicorns are normally distributed. A simple random sample of 18 unicorns has a standard deviation of 24.1. Find an 80% confidence interval for the standard deviation of the whole population: the lower confidence limit (LCL) and the upper confidence limit (UCL).

    Answer

    LCL \(\approx 19.97\), UCL \(\approx 31.29\)

    Solution

    Here \(n-1=17\) and \(\alpha=0.20\). The critical values have areas 0.10 and 0.90 to their right: \(\chi^2_R=24.769\) and \(\chi^2_L=10.085\). The confidence interval for \(\sigma\) is

    Note that the larger critical value gives the lower limit.
