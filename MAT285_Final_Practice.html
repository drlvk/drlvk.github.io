  1. Find the domain of the function \(K(x)=\dfrac{x+5}{\sqrt{12-3x}}\). Give your answer in interval notation.

    Answer

    \((-\infty,4)\)

    Solution

    The square root is defined only when \(12-3x\ge 0\). Since the square root is in the denominator, it also cannot be 0. So we need \(12-3x\gt 0\), that is, \(3x\lt 12\), or \(x\lt 4\). The numerator \(x+5\) is defined for every \(x\), so it adds no restriction. The domain is \((-\infty,4)\); the endpoint 4 is excluded.

  2. An open box will be made by cutting a square from each corner of a 6 ft by 16 ft piece of cardboard and then folding up the sides. What size square should be cut from each corner to produce a box of maximum volume? What is the maximum volume?

    Answer

    Squares with side \(\tfrac43\) ft; maximum volume \(\tfrac{1600}{27}\approx 59.3\) ft³

    Solution

    If squares of side \(x\) are cut out, the box has height \(x\) and a base of \(16-2x\) by \(6-2x\), so

    \[V(x)=x(16-2x)(6-2x)=4x^3-44x^2+96x,\qquad 0\le x\le 3.\]

    \(V'(x)=12x^2-88x+96=4(3x-4)(x-6)\) is zero at \(x=\tfrac43\) and \(x=6\); only \(x=\tfrac43\) is in \([0,3]\). Since \(V(0)=V(3)=0\) and \(V\left(\tfrac43\right)=\tfrac43\cdot\tfrac{40}{3}\cdot\tfrac{10}{3}=\tfrac{1600}{27}\), the maximum volume occurs at \(x=\tfrac43\).

  3. Find the derivatives in (a)–(c), and answer (d).

    1. \(y=4x\sec 6x\)
    2. \(y=6e^{\cos 4x}\)
    3. \(y=\cos^7x\)
    4. Find the slope of the tangent line to \(y=6\cos x-5\) at the point with \(x=-\tfrac{\pi}{4}\).
    Answer

    (a) \(4\sec 6x+24x\sec 6x\tan 6x\)    (b) \(-24\sin 4x\,e^{\cos 4x}\)    (c) \(-7\cos^6x\sin x\)    (d) \(3\sqrt2\approx 4.24\)

    Solution

    (a) Use the product rule, with \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\sec 6x=6\sec 6x\tan 6x\): \(y'=4\sec 6x+4x\cdot 6\sec 6x\tan 6x\).

    (b) By the chain rule, \(y'=6e^{\cos 4x}\cdot(-\sin 4x)\cdot 4=-24\sin 4x\,e^{\cos 4x}\).

    (c) \(y=(\cos x)^7\), so \(y'=7(\cos x)^6\cdot(-\sin x)=-7\cos^6x\sin x\).

    (d) \(y'=-6\sin x\) and \(\sin\left(-\tfrac{\pi}{4}\right)=-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\), so the slope is \(-6\cdot\left(-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\right)=3\sqrt2\).

  4. Find the indefinite integrals, using substitution.

    1. \(\displaystyle\int 9(3x+6)^4\,dx\)
    2. \(\displaystyle\int\frac{x^3}{x^4+2}\,dx\)
    3. \(\displaystyle\int x\sin(x^2)\,dx\)
    Answer

    (a) \(\tfrac35(3x+6)^5+C\)    (b) \(\tfrac14\ln(x^4+2)+C\)    (c) \(-\tfrac12\cos(x^2)+C\)

    Solution

    (a) Let \(u=3x+6\), so \(du=3\,dx\): \(\displaystyle\int 9u^4\cdot\tfrac13\,du=\int 3u^4\,du=\tfrac35u^5+C\).

    (b) Let \(u=x^4+2\), so \(du=4x^3\,dx\): \(\displaystyle\int\frac1u\cdot\tfrac14\,du=\tfrac14\ln|u|+C\). Here \(u\gt 0\), so the absolute value can be dropped.

    (c) Let \(u=x^2\), so \(du=2x\,dx\): \(\displaystyle\int\sin u\cdot\tfrac12\,du=-\tfrac12\cos u+C\).

  5. A bacteria culture grows according to \(N(t)=500e^{kt}\), where \(t\) is measured in hours. The culture doubles in size every 3 hours.

    1. Find \(k\).
    2. When will the culture reach 8000 bacteria?
    3. Find the average rate of change of \(N\) over the first 6 hours.
    Answer

    (a) \(k=\dfrac{\ln 2}{3}\approx 0.231\)    (b) after 12 hours    (c) 250 bacteria per hour

    Solution

    (a) \(N(3)=2N(0)\) gives \(500e^{3k}=1000\), so \(e^{3k}=2\), \(3k=\ln 2\), and \(k=\tfrac13\ln 2\approx 0.231\).

    (b) Solve \(500e^{kt}=8000\): \(e^{kt}=16\), so \(t=\dfrac{\ln 16}{k}=\dfrac{3\ln 16}{\ln 2}=\dfrac{3\cdot 4\ln 2}{\ln 2}=12\) hours. (Since \(16=2^4\), this is four doublings.)

    (c) \(N(6)=500e^{6k}=500\left(e^{3k}\right)^2=2000\), so the average rate of change is \(\dfrac{N(6)-N(0)}{6-0}=\dfrac{2000-500}{6}=250\) bacteria per hour.

  6. Let \(f(x)=2x^3+3x^2-36x+5\).

    1. Find an equation of the tangent line to the graph of \(f\) at \(x=1\).
    2. Find all values of \(x\) where the tangent line to the graph of \(f\) is horizontal.
    Answer

    (a) \(y=-24x-2\)    (b) \(x=-3\) and \(x=2\)

    Solution

    \(f'(x)=6x^2+6x-36\).

    (a) \(f(1)=2+3-36+5=-26\) and \(f'(1)=6+6-36=-24\). The tangent line is \(y-(-26)=-24(x-1)\), that is, \(y=-24x-2\).

    (b) The tangent line is horizontal where \(f'(x)=0\): \(6(x^2+x-6)=6(x+3)(x-2)=0\), so \(x=-3\) or \(x=2\).

  7. An area near Onondaga Lake has become contaminated with phosphorus. A proposed treatment plan will reduce the contamination for a while, but then it will build up again. The percent of phosphorus in the soil \(x\) months after the treatment begins is \[f(x)=\frac{x^2+25}{4x},\qquad 1\le x\le 12.\]

    1. When will the phosphorus be reduced to a minimum? What is the minimum percent?
    2. What is the maximum percent during this period, and when does it occur?
    Answer

    (a) After 5 months; the minimum is 2.5%    (b) 6.5%, at \(x=1\)

    Solution

    Write \(f(x)=\tfrac14x+\tfrac{25}{4}x^{-1}\). Then \(f'(x)=\tfrac14-\dfrac{25}{4x^2}\), which is zero when \(x^2=25\). The only critical number in \([1,12]\) is \(x=5\). Compare the values:

    \[f(1)=\frac{26}{4}=6.5,\qquad f(5)=\frac{50}{20}=2.5,\qquad f(12)=\frac{169}{48}\approx 3.52.\]

    The minimum is 2.5%, after 5 months. The maximum is 6.5%, at the start of the period (\(x=1\)).

  8. Use the product and quotient rules.

    1. Let \(f(t)=(t^2+3t+4)(3t^2+5)\). Find \(f'(t)\).
    2. Find \(f'(x)\) for \(f(x)=\dfrac{x^2-1}{x^2+3}\).
    3. Find an equation of the tangent line to \(y=\dfrac{2x+1}{x+2}\) at the point \((1,1)\).
    Answer

    (a) \((2t+3)(3t^2+5)+6t(t^2+3t+4)\), or \(12t^3+27t^2+34t+15\)    (b) \(\dfrac{8x}{(x^2+3)^2}\)    (c) \(y=\tfrac13x+\tfrac23\)

    Solution

    (a) By the product rule, \(f'(t)=(2t+3)(3t^2+5)+(t^2+3t+4)(6t)\). Expanding gives \((6t^3+9t^2+10t+15)+(6t^3+18t^2+24t)=12t^3+27t^2+34t+15\).

    (b) By the quotient rule,

    \[f'(x)=\frac{2x(x^2+3)-(x^2-1)(2x)}{(x^2+3)^2}=\frac{2x^3+6x-2x^3+2x}{(x^2+3)^2}=\frac{8x}{(x^2+3)^2}.\]

    (c) \(y'=\dfrac{2(x+2)-(2x+1)(1)}{(x+2)^2}=\dfrac{3}{(x+2)^2}\), so the slope at \(x=1\) is \(\tfrac39=\tfrac13\). The tangent line is \(y-1=\tfrac13(x-1)\), that is, \(y=\tfrac13x+\tfrac23\).

  9. Oil is leaking out of a ruptured tanker at the rate of \(r(t)=50e^{-0.02t}\) thousand liters per minute, where \(t\) is measured in minutes.

    1. At what rate is the oil leaking out at \(t=0\)? At \(t=50\)?
    2. How much oil leaks out during the first 50 minutes?
    Answer

    (a) 50 and \(50e^{-1}\approx 18.4\) thousand liters per minute    (b) \(2500(1-e^{-1})\approx 1580\) thousand liters

    Solution

    (a) \(r(0)=50e^0=50\) and \(r(50)=50e^{-1}\approx 18.4\).

    (b) The total amount is the integral of the rate:

    \[\int_0^{50}50e^{-0.02t}\,dt=\left[\frac{50}{-0.02}e^{-0.02t}\right]_0^{50}=-2500e^{-1}+2500=2500(1-e^{-1})\approx 1580.3.\]

  10. Let \(f(x)=x^2+3\) and \(g(x)=1-2x\). Find each of the following.

    1. \(f(g(x))\)
    2. \(g(f(x))\)
    3. \(f(g(3))\)
    Answer

    (a) \(4x^2-4x+4\)    (b) \(-2x^2-5\)    (c) \(28\)

    Solution

    (a) \(f(g(x))=(1-2x)^2+3=1-4x+4x^2+3=4x^2-4x+4\).

    (b) \(g(f(x))=1-2(x^2+3)=-2x^2-5\).

    (c) \(g(3)=-5\), so \(f(g(3))=f(-5)=25+3=28\). This agrees with (a) at \(x=3\): \(36-12+4=28\).

  11. The graph below shows the derivative \(f'\) of a function \(f\), not \(f\) itself. The graph of \(f'\) touches the \(x\)-axis at \(x=-1\), crosses it at \(x=2\), and has horizontal tangent lines at \(x=-1\) and \(x=1\).

    1. On which intervals is \(f\) increasing? Decreasing?
    2. At which \(x\)-values does \(f\) have a relative maximum? A relative minimum?
    3. On which intervals is \(f\) concave up? Concave down?
    4. Find the \(x\)-coordinates of the inflection points of \(f\).
    Answer

    (a) Increasing on \((2,\infty)\); decreasing on \((-\infty,-1)\) and \((-1,2)\), that is, on \((-\infty,2)\)
    (b) No relative maximum; relative minimum at \(x=2\)
    (c) Concave up on \((-\infty,-1)\) and \((1,\infty)\); concave down on \((-1,1)\)
    (d) \(x=-1\) and \(x=1\)

    Solution

    (a) \(f\) increases where \(f'\gt 0\) (graph above the axis) and decreases where \(f'\lt 0\) (graph below the axis).

    (b) \(f'\) changes from negative to positive at \(x=2\), so \(f\) has a relative minimum there. At \(x=-1\), \(f'=0\) but \(f'\) does not change sign (it is negative on both sides), so \(x=-1\) is a critical number that is neither a maximum nor a minimum.

    (c) \(f\) is concave up where \(f'\) is increasing and concave down where \(f'\) is decreasing. The graph of \(f'\) rises until \(x=-1\), falls from \(x=-1\) to \(x=1\), and rises after \(x=1\).

    (d) The concavity changes at the turning points of \(f'\), \(x=-1\) and \(x=1\).

  12. Find the equation of each line, in slope-intercept form.

    1. The line through the point \((6,1)\) that is parallel to the line \(2x-3y=6\).
    2. The line through the points \((-2,5)\) and \((4,-7)\).
    Answer

    (a) \(y=\tfrac23x-3\)    (b) \(y=-2x+1\)

    Solution

    (a) Solving \(2x-3y=6\) for \(y\) gives \(y=\tfrac23x-2\), so that line has slope \(\tfrac23\). A parallel line has the same slope: \(y-1=\tfrac23(x-6)\), that is, \(y=\tfrac23x-3\).

    (b) The slope is \(m=\dfrac{-7-5}{4-(-2)}=\dfrac{-12}{6}=-2\). Using the point \((-2,5)\): \(y-5=-2(x+2)\), that is, \(y=-2x+1\). (Check with the other point: \(-2(4)+1=-7\).)

  13. Use the definition of the derivative to find \(f'(x)\) for \(f(x)=\dfrac{4}{x+2}\).

    Answer

    \(f'(x)=-\dfrac{4}{(x+2)^2}\)

    Solution

    Bring the difference to a common denominator:

    \[f(x+h)-f(x)=\frac{4}{x+h+2}-\frac{4}{x+2}=\frac{4(x+2)-4(x+h+2)}{(x+h+2)(x+2)}=\frac{-4h}{(x+h+2)(x+2)}.\]

    Divide by \(h\), then let \(h\to 0\):

    \[f'(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{-4}{(x+h+2)(x+2)}=-\frac{4}{(x+2)^2}.\]

  14. Find the derivative of each function.

    1. \(f(x)=8\sqrt{x}-\dfrac{10}{\sqrt{x}}\)
    2. \(g(x)=\dfrac{x^2-3x+3}{x^2}\) (Hint: simplify first.)
    Answer

    (a) \(f'(x)=4x^{-1/2}+5x^{-3/2}\)    (b) \(g'(x)=3x^{-2}-6x^{-3}\)

    Solution

    (a) Write \(f(x)=8x^{1/2}-10x^{-1/2}\). By the power rule, \(f'(x)=8\cdot\tfrac12x^{-1/2}-10\cdot\left(-\tfrac12\right)x^{-3/2}=4x^{-1/2}+5x^{-3/2}\).

    (b) Divide each term by \(x^2\): \(g(x)=1-3x^{-1}+3x^{-2}\). Then \(g'(x)=3x^{-2}-6x^{-3}=\dfrac{3}{x^2}-\dfrac{6}{x^3}\).

  15. Find the absolute maximum and absolute minimum values of \(f(x)=x^3-6x^2+9x+2\) on the interval \([-1,4]\), and all values of \(x\) where they occur.

    Answer

    Absolute maximum \(6\), at \(x=1\) and \(x=4\); absolute minimum \(-14\), at \(x=-1\)

    Solution

    \(f'(x)=3x^2-12x+9=3(x-1)(x-3)\), so the critical numbers are \(x=1\) and \(x=3\), both in \([-1,4]\). Compare the values of \(f\) at the critical numbers and at the endpoints:

    \[f(-1)=-14,\qquad f(1)=6,\qquad f(3)=2,\qquad f(4)=6.\]

    The largest value is 6, attained at two points; the smallest is \(-14\).

  16. The number \(x\) of speakers a retail chain is willing to sell per week at a price of \(p\) dollars is \(x=78\sqrt{p+26}-370\). Find the supply and the instantaneous rate of change of supply when the price is $74.

    Answer

    Supply: 410 speakers per week. Rate of change: 3.9 speakers per dollar.

    Solution

    When \(p=74\), \(\sqrt{p+26}=\sqrt{100}=10\), so \(x=78\cdot 10-370=410\).

    By the chain rule, \(\dfrac{dx}{dp}=78\cdot\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{p+26}}=\dfrac{39}{\sqrt{p+26}}\), which equals \(\dfrac{39}{10}=3.9\) when \(p=74\). So supply increases by about 3.9 speakers per week for each dollar of price increase.

  17. Let \(f(x)=3x^2-5x\).

    1. State the definition of the derivative \(f'(x)\). (It involves a limit.)
    2. Use the definition of the derivative to find \(f'(x)\). (Use the definition, not the derivative rules.)
    Answer

    (a) \(f'(x)=\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\)    (b) \(f'(x)=6x-5\)

    Solution

    (b) Expand: \(f(x+h)=3(x+h)^2-5(x+h)=3x^2+6xh+3h^2-5x-5h\). Subtracting \(f(x)=3x^2-5x\) leaves \(f(x+h)-f(x)=6xh+3h^2-5h\). So

    \[f'(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{6xh+3h^2-5h}{h}=\lim_{h\to 0}(6x+3h-5)=6x-5.\]

  18. Consider the curve \(x^3+2xy+y^4=13\).

    1. Use implicit differentiation to find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\).
    2. Find an equation of the tangent line to the curve at the point \((2,1)\).
    Answer

    (a) \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac{3x^2+2y}{2x+4y^3}\)    (b) \(y-1=-\tfrac74(x-2)\), that is, \(y=-\tfrac74x+\tfrac92\)

    Solution

    (a) Differentiate both sides with respect to \(x\), using the product rule on \(2xy\) and the chain rule on \(y^4\):

    \[3x^2+2y+2x\frac{dy}{dx}+4y^3\frac{dy}{dx}=0,\qquad\text{so}\qquad(2x+4y^3)\frac{dy}{dx}=-(3x^2+2y).\]

    (b) The point is on the curve: \(8+4+1=13\). At \((2,1)\), \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac{12+2}{4+4}=-\dfrac74\).

  19. Evaluate the definite integrals.

    1. \(\displaystyle\int_0^3\left(x^2-4x+5\right)dx\)
    2. \(\displaystyle\int_1^4\left(3\sqrt{x}-\frac{4}{x^2}\right)dx\)
    3. \(\displaystyle\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}2\cos x\,dx\)
    Answer

    (a) \(6\)    (b) \(11\)    (c) \(4\)

    Solution

    (a) \(\left[\tfrac13x^3-2x^2+5x\right]_0^3=(9-18+15)-0=6\).

    (b) An antiderivative of \(3x^{1/2}-4x^{-2}\) is \(2x^{3/2}+4x^{-1}\), so the integral is \(\left[2x^{3/2}+\tfrac4x\right]_1^4=(16+1)-(2+4)=11\).

    (c) \(\left[2\sin x\right]_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}=2(1)-2(-1)=4\).

  20. Find the derivative of each function.

    1. \(y=(5x^3-6x)e^{-2x}\)
    2. \(y=6^{x^2+1}\)
    3. \(y=\dfrac{8e^x+5e^{-x}}{x}\)
    Answer

    (a) \((15x^2-6)e^{-2x}-2(5x^3-6x)e^{-2x}\)    (b) \(2x\ln 6\cdot 6^{x^2+1}\)    (c) \(\dfrac{(8e^x-5e^{-x})x-(8e^x+5e^{-x})}{x^2}\)

    Solution

    (a) Use the product rule, with \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{-2x}=-2e^{-2x}\) by the chain rule: \(y'=(15x^2-6)e^{-2x}+(5x^3-6x)(-2e^{-2x})\). This can also be written as \(e^{-2x}(-10x^3+15x^2+12x-6)\).

    (b) Since \(\dfrac{d}{du}6^u=6^u\ln 6\), the chain rule with \(u=x^2+1\) gives \(y'=6^{x^2+1}\ln 6\cdot 2x\).

    (c) Use the quotient rule, with \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(8e^x+5e^{-x}\right)=8e^x-5e^{-x}\):

  21. Find the indefinite integrals in (a)–(c), and solve (d).

    1. \(\displaystyle\int\frac{5x^3-9}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx\)
    2. \(\displaystyle\int\left(\frac4x-9e^{-0.3x}\right)dx\)
    3. \(\displaystyle\int\left(6\cos x+4\sec^2x\right)dx\)
    4. Find the function \(f(x)\) such that \(f'(x)=9x^2-4x+5\) and \(f(2)=24\).
    Answer

    (a) \(\tfrac{10}{7}x^{7/2}-18x^{1/2}+C\)    (b) \(4\ln|x|+30e^{-0.3x}+C\)    (c) \(6\sin x+4\tan x+C\)    (d) \(f(x)=3x^3-2x^2+5x-2\)

    Solution

    (a) Divide first: \(\dfrac{5x^3-9}{x^{1/2}}=5x^{5/2}-9x^{-1/2}\). Then \(\displaystyle\int\left(5x^{5/2}-9x^{-1/2}\right)dx=5\cdot\frac{x^{7/2}}{7/2}-9\cdot\frac{x^{1/2}}{1/2}+C=\tfrac{10}{7}x^{7/2}-18x^{1/2}+C\).

    (b) \(\displaystyle\int\frac4x\,dx=4\ln|x|\) and \(\displaystyle\int e^{-0.3x}\,dx=\frac{e^{-0.3x}}{-0.3}\), so the integral is \(4\ln|x|-\dfrac{9}{-0.3}e^{-0.3x}+C=4\ln|x|+30e^{-0.3x}+C\).

    (c) Since \((\sin x)'=\cos x\) and \((\tan x)'=\sec^2x\), the integral is \(6\sin x+4\tan x+C\).

    (d) \(f(x)=3x^3-2x^2+5x+C\). Then \(f(2)=24-8+10+C=26+C=24\) gives \(C=-2\).

  22. A 13-foot ladder is leaning against a vertical wall. The top of the ladder slides down the wall at a rate of 2 ft/s. How fast is the foot of the ladder moving away from the wall when the top is 12 feet above the ground?

    Answer

    \(\tfrac{24}{5}=4.8\) ft/s

    Solution

    Let \(x\) be the distance from the wall to the foot of the ladder, and \(y\) the height of the top. Then \(x^2+y^2=169\), so \(2x\dfrac{dx}{dt}+2y\dfrac{dy}{dt}=0\).

    When \(y=12\), \(x=\sqrt{169-144}=5\). The height is decreasing, so \(\dfrac{dy}{dt}=-2\). Then \(5\dfrac{dx}{dt}+12(-2)=0\), and \(\dfrac{dx}{dt}=\dfrac{24}{5}=4.8\) ft/s.

  23. Evaluate each limit. Use \(\infty\) or \(-\infty\) where appropriate.

    1. \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{(2-x)(3+4x)}{(1-2x)(5+x)}\)
    2. \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{x^3-4x+2}{x^4+3x^2}\)
    3. \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 4^-}\frac{x}{x^2-16}\)
    Answer

    (a) \(2\)    (b) \(0\)    (c) \(-\infty\)

    Solution

    (a) The numerator and denominator both have degree 2. Their leading terms are \((-x)(4x)=-4x^2\) and \((-2x)(x)=-2x^2\), so the limit is \(\dfrac{-4}{-2}=2\).

    (b) Divide the numerator and denominator by \(x^4\): \(\dfrac{1/x-4/x^3+2/x^4}{1+3/x^2}\to\dfrac{0}{1}=0\). (The denominator has the higher degree.)

    (c) As \(x\to 4^-\), the numerator approaches 4, while \(x^2-16\to 0\) through negative values (\(x^2\lt 16\) for \(x\) slightly less than 4). A positive number divided by a small negative number is large and negative, so the limit is \(-\infty\).

  24. Find the derivative of each function.

    1. \(y=(6x+2)\ln(4x-1)\)
    2. \(y=\log_8\sqrt{2x+5}\)
    3. \(f(x)=\ln\left[\dfrac{x^3\sqrt{x+1}}{(x^2+5)^2}\right]\) (Suggestion: rewrite before differentiating.)
    Answer

    (a) \(6\ln(4x-1)+\dfrac{4(6x+2)}{4x-1}\)    (b) \(\dfrac{1}{(2x+5)\ln 8}\)    (c) \(\dfrac3x+\dfrac{1}{2(x+1)}-\dfrac{4x}{x^2+5}\)

    Solution

    (a) Use the product rule, with \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln(4x-1)=\dfrac{4}{4x-1}\): \(y'=6\ln(4x-1)+(6x+2)\cdot\dfrac{4}{4x-1}\).

    (b) Rewrite \(y=\tfrac12\log_8(2x+5)\). Since \(\dfrac{d}{du}\log_8 u=\dfrac{1}{u\ln 8}\), \(y'=\tfrac12\cdot\dfrac{2}{(2x+5)\ln 8}=\dfrac{1}{(2x+5)\ln 8}\).

    (c) By the properties of logarithms, \(f(x)=3\ln x+\tfrac12\ln(x+1)-2\ln(x^2+5)\), so

    \[f'(x)=\frac3x+\frac{1}{2(x+1)}-2\cdot\frac{2x}{x^2+5}=\frac3x+\frac{1}{2(x+1)}-\frac{4x}{x^2+5}.\]

  25. Let \(f(x)=3-3x^2\).

    1. Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^2f(x)\,dx\).
    2. Find the area between the graph of \(f\) and the \(x\)-axis on the interval \([0,2]\). (Check whether the graph crosses the \(x\)-axis.)
    Answer

    (a) \(-2\)    (b) \(6\)

    Solution

    An antiderivative is \(F(x)=3x-x^3\).

    (a) \(F(2)-F(0)=(6-8)-0=-2\).

    (b) \(f(x)=3(1-x)(1+x)\) changes sign at \(x=1\): \(f\ge 0\) on \([0,1]\) and \(f\le 0\) on \([1,2]\). So the area is

    \[\int_0^1f(x)\,dx-\int_1^2f(x)\,dx=\left[F(1)-F(0)\right]-\left[F(2)-F(1)\right]=2-(-4)=6.\]

    The integral in (a) is smaller than the area, because the part below the axis counts as negative.

  26. Let \(f(x)=x^2e^{-x}\).

    1. Find the critical numbers of \(f\).
    2. Use the second derivative test to decide whether each critical number gives a relative maximum or a relative minimum. Find the value of \(f\) there.
    Answer

    (a) \(x=0\) and \(x=2\)    (b) relative minimum \(f(0)=0\); relative maximum \(f(2)=4e^{-2}\approx 0.541\)

    Solution

    (a) By the product rule, \(f'(x)=2xe^{-x}-x^2e^{-x}=x(2-x)e^{-x}\). Since \(e^{-x}\gt 0\), \(f'(x)=0\) only at \(x=0\) and \(x=2\).
