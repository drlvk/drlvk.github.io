Final exam practice

  1. Suppose that \(f(2)=8\), \(f'(2)=3\), \(g(2)=4\), and \(g'(2)=-2\).

    1. Let \(H(x)=f(x)\,g(x)\). Find \(H'(2)\).
    2. Let \(Q(x)=\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}\). Find \(Q'(2)\).
    3. Find the equation of the line tangent to the graph of \(y=Q(x)\) at \(x=2\).
    Answer

    (a) \(-4\)    (b) \(\tfrac74\)    (c) \(y=\tfrac74x-\tfrac32\)

    Solution

    (a) Product rule: \(H'(2)=f'(2)g(2)+f(2)g'(2)=3\cdot 4+8\cdot(-2)=-4\).

    (b) Quotient rule:

    \[Q'(2)=\frac{f'(2)g(2)-f(2)g'(2)}{g(2)^2}=\frac{3\cdot 4-8\cdot(-2)}{4^2}=\frac{28}{16}=\frac74.\]

    (c) \(Q(2)=\dfrac84=2\), so the tangent line is \(y-2=\tfrac74(x-2)\), that is, \(y=\tfrac74x-\tfrac32\).

  2. Answer each question about continuous compounding.

    1. $6,000 is invested at 4.5% compounded continuously. Find its value after 10 years, to the nearest dollar.
    2. How long does it take the investment in (a) to double? Round to the nearest tenth of a year.
    3. An investment of $5,000 grows to $15,000 in 20 years with continuous compounding. Find the annual rate, to the nearest hundredth of a percent.
    Answer

    (a) $9,410    (b) 15.4 years    (c) \(r=\dfrac{\ln 3}{20}\approx 5.49\%\)

    Solution

    (a) \(A=Pe^{rt}=6000e^{0.045\cdot 10}=6000e^{0.45}\approx 9409.87\).

    (b) Solve \(6000e^{0.045t}=12000\): \(e^{0.045t}=2\), so \(t=\dfrac{\ln 2}{0.045}\approx 15.4\) years.

    (c) \(15000=5000e^{20r}\) gives \(e^{20r}=3\), so \(r=\dfrac{\ln 3}{20}\approx 0.0549=5.49\%\).

  3. The marginal revenue function for a product is \(R'(x)=80+6x-0.3x^2\), where \(x\) is the number of units sold. Find the revenue function \(R(x)\), and then the price-demand equation.

    Answer

    \(R(x)=80x+3x^2-0.1x^3\); price-demand equation \(p=80+3x-0.1x^2\)

    Solution

    Antidifferentiate: \(R(x)=80x+3x^2-0.1x^3+K\). Since \(R(0)=0\), \(K=0\).

    Revenue is price times quantity, \(R=xp\), so \(p=\dfrac{R(x)}{x}=80+3x-0.1x^2\).

  4. Let \(f(x)=\dfrac{2x-4}{x+3}\).

    1. Find the equation of the vertical asymptote of \(f\).
    2. Find and simplify \(f(x+2)\).
    3. Find \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)\).
    Answer

    (a) \(x=-3\)    (b) \(\dfrac{2x}{x+5}\)    (c) \(2\)

    Solution

    (a) The denominator is zero at \(x=-3\), and the numerator is not (it equals \(-10\) there), so the vertical asymptote is \(x=-3\).

    (b) \(f(x+2)=\dfrac{2(x+2)-4}{(x+2)+3}=\dfrac{2x}{x+5}\).

    (c) The numerator and denominator have the same degree, so the limit is the ratio of the leading coefficients: \(\tfrac21=2\).

  5. Use a sign chart to solve each inequality. Write the answer in interval notation.

    1. \(x^2-2x-15\lt 0\)
    2. \(\dfrac{x^2-4}{x-3}\ge 0\)
    Answer

    (a) \((-3,5)\)    (b) \([-2,2]\cup(3,\infty)\)

    Solution

    (a) \(x^2-2x-15=(x-5)(x+3)\), with partition numbers \(-3\) and \(5\). Test points: \(x=-4\) gives \(9\gt 0\), \(x=0\) gives \(-15\lt 0\), \(x=6\) gives \(9\gt 0\). The expression is negative on \((-3,5)\); the endpoints are excluded because the inequality is strict.

    (b) The numerator is zero at \(x=\pm2\) (included, since the inequality is \(\ge\)); the denominator is zero at \(x=3\) (excluded). Test points: \(x=-3\) gives \(-\tfrac56\lt 0\); \(x=0\) gives \(\tfrac43\gt 0\); \(x=2.5\) gives \(-4.5\lt 0\); \(x=4\) gives \(12\gt 0\). So the solution set is \([-2,2]\cup(3,\infty)\).

  6. Answer each question about tangent lines.

    1. Find the equation of the line tangent to \(y=3\ln x+x^2\) at \(x=1\).
    2. Let \(f(x)=2x^3+3x^2-12x+5\). Find the equation of the tangent line at \(x=2\), and find all values of \(x\) where the tangent line is horizontal.
    Answer

    (a) \(y=5x-4\)    (b) \(y=24x-39\); horizontal at \(x=-2\) and \(x=1\)

    Solution

    (a) At \(x=1\), \(y=3\ln 1+1=1\). Since \(y'=\dfrac3x+2x\), the slope is \(3+2=5\). The tangent line is \(y-1=5(x-1)\), that is, \(y=5x-4\).

    (b) \(f'(x)=6x^2+6x-12\). At \(x=2\): \(f(2)=16+12-24+5=9\) and \(f'(2)=24+12-12=24\), so the tangent line is \(y-9=24(x-2)\), that is, \(y=24x-39\). The tangent line is horizontal where \(f'(x)=0\): \(6(x^2+x-2)=6(x+2)(x-1)=0\), so \(x=-2\) or \(x=1\).

  7. Solve each equation for \(x\). Give exact answers.

    1. \(3\cdot 10^{2x}-5=40\)
    2. \(4e^{3x-1}=20\)
    3. \(\log_3x+\log_3(x-8)=2\)
    Answer

    (a) \(x=\dfrac{\log 15}{2}\approx 0.588\)    (b) \(x=\dfrac{1+\ln 5}{3}\approx 0.870\)    (c) \(x=9\)

    Solution

    (a) \(3\cdot 10^{2x}=45\), so \(10^{2x}=15\). Taking the common logarithm, \(2x=\log 15\), so \(x=\tfrac12\log 15\).

    (b) \(e^{3x-1}=5\). Taking \(\ln\), \(3x-1=\ln 5\), so \(x=\dfrac{1+\ln 5}{3}\).

    (c) Combine the logarithms: \(\log_3\bigl(x(x-8)\bigr)=2\), so \(x(x-8)=3^2=9\). Then \(x^2-8x-9=(x-9)(x+1)=0\). Reject \(x=-1\), because \(\log_3(-1)\) is undefined. So \(x=9\).

  8. Let \[f(x)=\begin{cases}6, & x\lt 2\\ 3x, & 2\le x\le 4\\ x^2-2, & x\gt 4.\end{cases}\] Find \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 2^-}f(x)\), \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 2^+}f(x)\), \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 4^-}f(x)\), and \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 4^+}f(x)\). Then find all points of discontinuity of \(f\), and explain.

    Answer

    \(6\), \(6\), \(12\), \(14\); \(f\) is discontinuous only at \(x=4\)

    Solution

    Near \(x=2\): from the left \(f(x)=6\), and from the right \(3x\to 6\). Both one-sided limits equal \(f(2)=6\), so \(f\) is continuous at \(x=2\). Near \(x=4\): from the left \(3x\to 12\), and from the right \(x^2-2\to 14\). The one-sided limits differ, so \(f\) is discontinuous at \(x=4\). Each formula is continuous on its own interval, so there are no other discontinuities.

  9. A store has 120 units of a product in stock. The product now sells for $15 per unit; next month the price will rise to $18 per unit. The store wants the total revenue from all 120 units to be at least $1,956. What is the largest number of units it can sell this month? (State an inequality, then solve it.)

    Answer

    68 units

    Solution

    If \(x\) units are sold this month at $15, the remaining \(120-x\) units are sold next month at $18. The revenue condition is

    \[15x+18(120-x)\ge 1956,\qquad\text{that is,}\qquad 2160-3x\ge 1956.\]

    So \(-3x\ge -204\). Dividing by \(-3\) reverses the inequality: \(x\le 68\). At most 68 units can be sold this month.

  10. Let \(f(x)=x^4-4x^3-18x^2+7\).

    1. Find the second derivative \(f''(x)\).
    2. Find the intervals on which \(f\) is concave up and the intervals on which it is concave down. Then find the \(x\)-coordinates of the inflection points of \(f\).
    Answer

    (a) \(f''(x)=12x^2-24x-36\)    (b) Concave up on \((-\infty,-1)\) and \((3,\infty)\); concave down on \((-1,3)\). Inflection points at \(x=-1\) and \(x=3\).

    Solution

    (a) \(f'(x)=4x^3-12x^2-36x\) and \(f''(x)=12x^2-24x-36\).

    (b) \(f''(x)=12(x^2-2x-3)=12(x-3)(x+1)\), which is zero at \(x=-1\) and \(x=3\). Test points: \(f''(-2)=60\gt 0\), \(f''(0)=-36\lt 0\), \(f''(4)=60\gt 0\). The concavity changes at both points, so both are inflection points.

  11. The total cost and the total revenue, in dollars, for producing and selling \(x\) toasters are \[C(x)=10x+3000\qquad\text{and}\qquad R(x)=50x-0.1x^2.\]

    1. Find the value of \(x\) where the graph of \(R\) has a horizontal tangent line.
    2. Find the profit function \(P(x)\).
    3. Find the marginal profit at \(x=150\).
    4. Find the value of \(x\) where the graph of \(P\) has a horizontal tangent line.
    5. Find the break-even points.
    Answer

    (a) \(x=250\)    (b) \(P(x)=-0.1x^2+40x-3000\)    (c) $10    (d) \(x=200\)    (e) \(x=100\) and \(x=300\)

    Solution

    (a) \(R'(x)=50-0.2x=0\) gives \(x=250\).

    (b) \(P(x)=R(x)-C(x)=50x-0.1x^2-10x-3000=-0.1x^2+40x-3000\).

    (c) \(P'(x)=40-0.2x\), so \(P'(150)=10\): selling the 151st toaster adds about $10 to the profit.

    (d) \(P'(x)=0\) gives \(x=200\).

    (e) \(P(x)=0\): multiply by \(-10\) to get \(x^2-400x+30000=0\), which factors as \((x-100)(x-300)=0\).

  12. Find the derivative of each function. You do not need to simplify the answers.

    1. \(y=(5x-2)^7\)
    2. \(y=\sqrt{2x^3+5x}\)
    3. \(y=\left(x^2+\dfrac{3}{x}\right)^5\)
    4. \(y=\dfrac{1}{(x^2+2x+3)^3}\)
    Answer

    (a) \(35(5x-2)^6\)    (b) \(\dfrac{6x^2+5}{2\sqrt{2x^3+5x}}\)    (c) \(5\left(x^2+\dfrac3x\right)^4\left(2x-\dfrac{3}{x^2}\right)\)    (d) \(-\dfrac{3(2x+2)}{(x^2+2x+3)^4}\)

    Solution

    Each function is a power of an inner function \(u\), so \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\left[u^n\right]=nu^{n-1}u'\).

    (a) \(y'=7(5x-2)^6\cdot 5=35(5x-2)^6\).

    (b) \(y=(2x^3+5x)^{1/2}\), so \(y'=\tfrac12(2x^3+5x)^{-1/2}(6x^2+5)=\dfrac{6x^2+5}{2\sqrt{2x^3+5x}}\).

    (c) The inner function is \(x^2+3x^{-1}\), with derivative \(2x-3x^{-2}\): \(y'=5\left(x^2+\tfrac3x\right)^4\left(2x-\tfrac{3}{x^2}\right)\).

    (d) \(y=(x^2+2x+3)^{-3}\), so \(y'=-3(x^2+2x+3)^{-4}(2x+2)\).

  13. Find the indefinite integrals.

    1. \(\displaystyle\int\left(12x^3-6e^x+\frac12\right)dx\)
    2. \(\displaystyle\int\left(\frac4x+9e^x-7\right)dx\)
    3. \(\displaystyle\int\left(5\sqrt{x}+\frac{2}{x^3}-\frac3x\right)dx\)
    Answer

    (a) \(3x^4-6e^x+\tfrac12x+C\)    (b) \(4\ln|x|+9e^x-7x+C\)    (c) \(\tfrac{10}{3}x^{3/2}-x^{-2}-3\ln|x|+C\)

    Solution

    Use \(\displaystyle\int x^n\,dx=\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}+C\) for \(n\ne-1\), \(\displaystyle\int\frac1x\,dx=\ln|x|+C\), and \(\displaystyle\int e^x\,dx=e^x+C\).

    (a) \(12\cdot\dfrac{x^4}{4}-6e^x+\dfrac12x+C=3x^4-6e^x+\tfrac12x+C\).

    (b) \(4\ln|x|+9e^x-7x+C\).

    (c) Write the integrand as \(5x^{1/2}+2x^{-3}-3x^{-1}\): \(5\cdot\dfrac{x^{3/2}}{3/2}+2\cdot\dfrac{x^{-2}}{-2}-3\ln|x|+C=\tfrac{10}{3}x^{3/2}-x^{-2}-3\ln|x|+C\).

  14. The total cost of producing \(x\) coffee machines is \(C(x)=1800+50x-0.25x^2\) dollars.

    1. Find the exact cost of producing the 41st machine.
    2. Use marginal cost to approximate the cost of producing the 41st machine.
    Answer

    (a) $29.75    (b) $30

    Solution

    (a) The 41st machine raises production from 40 to 41, so its exact cost is

    \[C(41)-C(40)=(1800+2050-420.25)-(1800+2000-400)=3429.75-3400=29.75.\]

    (b) \(C'(x)=50-0.5x\). The marginal cost when 40 machines have already been made approximates the cost of the next one: \(C'(40)=50-20=30\).

  15. Find the derivative of each function. You do not need to simplify the answers.

    1. \(y=(2x^2+5)(x^3-4x)\)
    2. \(f(z)=\dfrac{3z+2}{z^2+4}\)
    3. \(g(x)=\dfrac{x^4-3x}{2x-e^x}\)
    Answer

    (a) \(4x(x^3-4x)+(2x^2+5)(3x^2-4)\), or \(10x^4-9x^2-20\)    (b) \(\dfrac{3(z^2+4)-(3z+2)(2z)}{(z^2+4)^2}\), or \(\dfrac{-3z^2-4z+12}{(z^2+4)^2}\)    (c) \(\dfrac{(4x^3-3)(2x-e^x)-(x^4-3x)(2-e^x)}{(2x-e^x)^2}\)

    Solution

    (a) Product rule: \(y'=4x(x^3-4x)+(2x^2+5)(3x^2-4)\). (Expanding first also works: \(y=2x^5-3x^3-20x\), so \(y'=10x^4-9x^2-20\).)

    (b) Quotient rule:

    \[f'(z)=\frac{3(z^2+4)-(3z+2)(2z)}{(z^2+4)^2}=\frac{-3z^2-4z+12}{(z^2+4)^2}.\]

    (c) Quotient rule, with \(\dfrac{d}{dx}(2x-e^x)=2-e^x\):

    \[g'(x)=\frac{(4x^3-3)(2x-e^x)-(x^4-3x)(2-e^x)}{(2x-e^x)^2}.\]

  16. Find the domain of each function. Give your answer in interval notation.

    1. \(g(x)=\dfrac{\sqrt{x+4}}{3x^2-15x}\)
    2. \(f(x)=\sqrt{6-2x}+\dfrac{x+1}{x-1}\)
    3. \(h(x)=\dfrac{\sqrt{x^2+9}}{2x^2-7x}\)
    Answer

    (a) \([-4,0)\cup(0,5)\cup(5,\infty)\)    (b) \((-\infty,1)\cup(1,3]\)    (c) \((-\infty,0)\cup\left(0,\tfrac72\right)\cup\left(\tfrac72,\infty\right)\)

    Solution

    A square root needs a nonnegative radicand, and a denominator cannot be 0.

    (a) \(x+4\ge 0\) gives \(x\ge -4\). The denominator \(3x(x-5)\) is zero at \(x=0\) and \(x=5\), so these are removed.

    (b) \(6-2x\ge 0\) gives \(x\le 3\), and the fraction needs \(x\ne 1\).

    (c) \(x^2+9\gt 0\) for every \(x\), so the square root adds no restriction. The denominator \(x(2x-7)\) is zero at \(x=0\) and \(x=\tfrac72\).

  17. For each price-demand equation, find the elasticity of demand at the given price, and describe the demand as elastic, inelastic, or having unit elasticity.

    1. \(x=\dfrac{5000}{p^{3/2}}\), at \(p=25\)
    2. \(x=100\left(64-p^2\right)\), at \(p=4\)
    Answer

    (a) \(E=\tfrac32\), elastic    (b) \(E=\tfrac23\), inelastic

    Solution

    Use \(E(p)=-\dfrac{p\,x'(p)}{x(p)}\).

    (a) \(x=5000p^{-3/2}\), so \(x'(p)=-7500p^{-5/2}\) and \(E(p)=\dfrac{7500p^{-3/2}}{5000p^{-3/2}}=\dfrac32\) at every price. Since \(\tfrac32\gt 1\), the demand is elastic.

    (b) \(x'(p)=-200p\), so \(E(p)=\dfrac{200p^2}{100\left(64-p^2\right)}=\dfrac{2p^2}{64-p^2}\). Then \(E(4)=\dfrac{32}{48}=\dfrac23\lt 1\): inelastic.

  18. Find the derivative of each function.

    1. \(y=6x\sqrt{x}\)
    2. \(y=\dfrac{2}{x^4}-\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{x}}+\sqrt[3]{x}\)
    3. \(y=\dfrac{3x^2+4}{2x}\)
    Answer

    (a) \(9\sqrt{x}\)    (b) \(-8x^{-5}+\tfrac52x^{-3/2}+\tfrac13x^{-2/3}\)    (c) \(\tfrac32-\dfrac{2}{x^2}\)

    Solution

    Rewrite each function as a sum of powers of \(x\), then use the power rule.

    (b) \(y=2x^{-4}-5x^{-1/2}+x^{1/3}\), so \(y'=-8x^{-5}+\tfrac52x^{-3/2}+\tfrac13x^{-2/3}\).

    (c) Divide each term by \(2x\): \(y=\tfrac32x+2x^{-1}\), so \(y'=\tfrac32-2x^{-2}\).

  19. Let \(f(x)=-x^3+3x^2+9x-4\). Answer the questions using calculus.

    1. Find \(f'(x)\) and the critical numbers of \(f\).
    2. Find the intervals on which \(f\) is increasing and the intervals on which it is decreasing.
    3. Find the \(x\)-coordinates of all local maxima and local minima.
    4. Find \(f''(x)\), the intervals on which \(f\) is concave up and concave down, and the \(x\)-coordinates of all inflection points.

    (a) \(f'(x)=-3x^2+6x+9\); critical numbers \(x=-1\) and \(x=3\)
    (b) Increasing on \((-1,3)\); decreasing on \((-\infty,-1)\) and \((3,\infty)\)
    (c) Local minimum at \(x=-1\); local maximum at \(x=3\)
    (d) \(f''(x)=-6x+6\); concave up on \((-\infty,1)\), concave down on \((1,\infty)\); inflection point at \(x=1\)

  20. Find the indefinite integrals.

    1. \(\displaystyle\int 6x\left(2-x^3\right)dx\)
    2. \(\displaystyle\int\frac{4x+3}{x^2}\,dx\)
    3. \(\displaystyle\int\frac{3x-4}{2x^3}\,dx\)
    4. \(\displaystyle\int x^2\left(3x+5x^3\right)dx\)
    Answer

    (a) \(6x^2-\tfrac65x^5+C\)    (b) \(4\ln|x|-\dfrac3x+C\)    (c) \(-\dfrac{3}{2x}+\dfrac{1}{x^2}+C\)    (d) \(\tfrac34x^4+\tfrac56x^6+C\)

    Solution

    (d) \(x^2(3x+5x^3)=3x^3+5x^5\), with antiderivative \(\tfrac34x^4+\tfrac56x^6\).

  21. Write \(\log\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{x^5}}{(x^2-9)^3}\right)\) in terms of \(\log x\), \(\log(x+3)\), and \(\log(x-3)\).

  22. Let \(f(x)=3x+\dfrac{48}{x}\).

    1. Find \(f'(x)\).
    2. Find the intervals on which \(f\) is increasing and the intervals on which it is decreasing. Then find the \(x\)-coordinates of all local maxima and local minima.
    Answer

    (a) \(f'(x)=3-\dfrac{48}{x^2}\)    (b) Increasing on \((-\infty,-4)\) and \((4,\infty)\); decreasing on \((-4,0)\) and \((0,4)\). Local maximum at \(x=-4\); local minimum at \(x=4\).

    Solution

    \(f(x)=3x+48x^{-1}\), so \(f'(x)=3-48x^{-2}=\dfrac{3(x^2-16)}{x^2}\). This is zero at \(x=\pm 4\), and undefined at \(x=0\), where \(f\) itself is undefined; the sign chart must be split there too.

    Test points: \(f'(-5)=1.08\gt 0\), \(f'(-1)=-45\lt 0\), \(f'(1)=-45\lt 0\), \(f'(5)=1.08\gt 0\). So \(f\) increases on \((-\infty,-4)\) and \((4,\infty)\), and decreases on \((-4,0)\) and \((0,4)\). There is a local maximum at \(x=-4\) and a local minimum at \(x=4\).

  23. The marginal cost function for a product is \(\dfrac{dC}{dx}=50+12x^{-1/2}+4e^x\), and the fixed cost is $800. Find the total cost function \(C(x)\).

    Answer

    \(C(x)=50x+24\sqrt{x}+4e^x+796\)

    Solution

    Antidifferentiate: \(C(x)=50x+12\cdot\dfrac{x^{1/2}}{1/2}+4e^x+K=50x+24\sqrt{x}+4e^x+K\).

    The fixed cost is \(C(0)=0+0+4e^0+K=4+K\). Setting \(4+K=800\) gives \(K=796\). (Note that the constant is not 800, because \(4e^x\) is not zero at \(x=0\).)

  24. The price-demand equation for a product is \(p=200-x^2\), and the price-supply equation is \(p=10x+56\), where \(x\ge 0\). Find the equilibrium quantity and the equilibrium price.

    Answer

    \(x=8\), \(p=\$136\)

    Solution

    Set supply equal to demand: \(200-x^2=10x+56\), so \(x^2+10x-144=0\), which factors as \((x+18)(x-8)=0\). The root \(x=-18\) is rejected because \(x\ge 0\). So \(x=8\) and \(p=10(8)+56=136\). (Check: \(200-8^2=136\).)

  25. Each unit of a product sells for a fixed price. For each cost function, find the quantity \(x\) that maximizes the profit, and the maximum profit. Your answer must be supported by calculus.

    1. \(C(x)=2x^{3/2}+14x+300\) dollars, and each unit sells for $50.
    2. \(C(x)=\dfrac{x^2}{4}+40x+800\) dollars, and each unit sells for $140.
    Answer

    (a) \(x=144\); maximum profit $1,428    (b) \(x=200\); maximum profit $9,200

    Solution

    (a) \(P(x)=50x-\left(2x^{3/2}+14x+300\right)=36x-2x^{3/2}-300\). Then \(P'(x)=36-3x^{1/2}\), which is zero when \(\sqrt{x}=12\), that is, \(x=144\). Since \(P''(x)=-\tfrac32x^{-1/2}\lt 0\) for \(x\gt 0\), this is a maximum. \(P(144)=36(144)-2(1728)-300=5184-3456-300=1428\).

    (b) \(P(x)=140x-\left(\tfrac{x^2}{4}+40x+800\right)=100x-\tfrac{x^2}{4}-800\). Then \(P'(x)=100-\tfrac{x}{2}=0\) gives \(x=200\), and \(P''(x)=-\tfrac12\lt 0\), so this is a maximum. \(P(200)=20000-10000-800=9200\).
