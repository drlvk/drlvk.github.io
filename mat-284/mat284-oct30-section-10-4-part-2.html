RecapThe chain rule so far

The chain rule says that the derivative of a composite function (say, \(y\) is a function of \(u\), which is a function of \(x\)) is the product of two intermediate derivatives:

\[(\text{outer})' \cdot (\text{inner})'\]

Find \(f'(x)\) for \(f(x) = (x^2 + 7)^4\), a composition with inner function \(x^2 + 7\).

Show solution
  1. Inner\(x^2 + 7\) has derivative \(2x\)
  2. Chain rule\(f'(x) = 4(x^2 + 7)^3 \cdot (2x) = 8x(x^2 + 7)^3\)

Answerf′(x) = 8x(x² + 7)³

Power chain rule

An often-used special case, when the outer function is raising to a power, as in the example above:

  • \((u^a)' = a u^{a-1} \cdot u'\),   that is, \(\big((\square)^a\big)' = a(\square)^{a-1} \cdot (\square)'\)

Common mistakes, on \(f(x) = (x^2 + 7)^4\)

(1) Forgetting \(u'\), which leads to \(f'(x) = 4(x^2 + 7)^3\). The factor \((2x)\) is missing.

(2) Plugging \(u'\) into the outer derivative: \(f'(x) = 4(2x)^3\). The \(2x\) should be multiplied by, not plugged in.

Compare to the correct form: \(f'(x) = 4(x^2 + 7)^3 \cdot (2x)\). In the correct formula, the inner function is unchanged in the first part, where the derivative is applied only to the outer function. The second part is just the derivative of the inner function. The two parts are multiplied together.

Special caseThe exponential chain rule

When the exponential function is composed with another, for example \(y = e^{3x^2 + 2}\), we need the chain rule to find its derivative. The order of operations is \(x \to 3x^2 + 2 \to e^{3x^2+2}\), so the inner function is \(3x^2 + 2\).

Find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) for \(y = e^{3x^2 + 2}\).

Show solution
  1. Name it\(y = e^u\), where \(u = 3x^2 + 2\)
  2. Chain rule\[\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du}\,\frac{du}{dx} = e^{3x^2+2} \cdot (3x^2 + 2)' = e^{3x^2+2} \cdot (6x) = 6x\,e^{3x^2+2}\]

Answer6x·e^(3x² + 2)

Worth memorizing

It may be easier to remember this as the exponential chain rule. The outer part is \(e^u\) again, since \(e^u\) is its own derivative:

  • \((e^u)' = e^u \cdot u'\),   that is, \(\left(e^{\square}\right)' = e^{\square} \cdot (\square)'\)
  • for other bases: \((b^u)' = b^u (\ln b) \cdot u'\)

Contrast

\((2^x)' = 2^x \ln 2\): no chain rule, because the exponent is just \(x\). But \(\left(2^{7-x^2}\right)' = 2^{7-x^2} \cdot \ln 2 \cdot (-2x)\): the same power of 2, then \(\ln(\text{base})\), then \((\text{inner})'\).

Some examples: find (a) \(\left(3^{4-5x}\right)'\) and (b) \(\left(e^{\sqrt{x}}\right)'\).

Show solution
  1. (a)\(4 - 5x\) is the inner function: \[3^{4-5x} \ln 3 \cdot (4 - 5x)' = -5\ln 3 \cdot 3^{4-5x}\]
  2. (b)\(\left(e^{\square}\right)' = e^{\square} \cdot (\square)'\) with \(\square = \sqrt{x}\): \[e^{\sqrt{x}} \cdot (\sqrt{x})' = e^{\sqrt{x}} \cdot \frac12 x^{-1/2} = \frac{e^{\sqrt{x}}}{2\sqrt{x}}\]

Answer(a) −5 ln 3 · 3⁴⁻⁵ˣ   (b) e^√x / (2√x)

Special caseThe logarithmic chain rule

When the logarithm is composed with another function, for example \(y = \ln(x^2 + 3)\), we need the chain rule to find its derivative. The inner function is \(x^2 + 3\).

Find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) for \(y = \ln(x^2 + 3)\).

Show solution
  1. Name it\(y = \ln u\), where \(u = x^2 + 3\)
  2. Chain rule(outer)′ is \(\frac1u\), (inner)′ is \(u'\): \[\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{u} \cdot u' = \frac{1}{x^2 + 3} \cdot (x^2 + 3)' = \frac{2x}{x^2 + 3}\]

Answer2x/(x² + 3)

An aside: ln is not multiplication

\(17(x^2 + 3) = 17x^2 + 51\) is multiplication, so the 17 distributes. But \(\ln(x^2 + 3)\) is the function “ln” applied to \(x^2 + 3\). Nothing distributes: \(\ln(x^2 + 3) \ne \ln x^2 + \ln 3\).

Worth memorizing

It may be easier to remember this as the logarithmic chain rule:

  • \((\ln u)' = \dfrac{1}{u} \cdot u' = \dfrac{u'}{u}\),   that is, \((\ln \square)' = \dfrac{1}{\square} \cdot (\square)'\)
  • for other bases: \((\log_b u)' = \dfrac{1}{u \ln b} \cdot u'\)

Find \(\left(\log(x^2 + 3)\right)'\). Here \(\log\) with no base written means base 10.

Show solution
  1. Chain rule\[\left(\log(x^2+3)\right)' = \underbrace{\frac{1}{(x^2 + 3)\ln 10}}_{(\text{outer})'} \cdot \underbrace{(2x)}_{(\text{inner})'}\]

Answer2x / ((x² + 3) ln 10)

Big pictureStrategy for derivatives, final version

The key to finding derivatives is the correct identification of a given function. The rows marked chain are the ones that use the chain rule.

Which rule to use
The function isIts derivative is
Sum or difference: \((\square) \pm (\square)\)\((\square)' \pm (\square)'\)
Something with a constant factor, like \(7 \cdot (\square)\)\(7 \cdot (\square)'\)
Just a constant, like \(7\)\(0\)
Power of the variable: \(x^a\) (\(a\) is a number)\(a x^{a-1}\)
Power of an expression: \((\square)^a\)  chain\(a(\square)^{a-1}(\square)'\)
Product: \((\square) \cdot (\blacksquare)\)\((\square)' \cdot (\blacksquare) + (\square) \cdot (\blacksquare)'\)
Quotient: \((\square) / (\blacksquare)\)\(\dfrac{(\square)' \cdot (\blacksquare) - (\square) \cdot (\blacksquare)'}{(\blacksquare)^2}\)
Logarithm of the variable: \(\ln x\)\(\dfrac{1}{x}\)
Logarithm of an expression: \(\ln(\square)\)  chain\(\dfrac{1}{(\square)} \cdot (\square)'\)
Exponential function: \(e^x\)\(e^x\)
Exponential of an expression: \(e^{\square}\)  chain\(e^{\square} \cdot (\square)'\)
General composition: \(f(\square)\)  chain\(f'(\square) \cdot (\square)'\)

Other bases

For bases \(b\) different from \(e\), include the logarithm of the base:

  • \((\log_b x)' = \dfrac{1}{x \ln b}\)     \((b^x)' = b^x \ln b\)

PracticeCombinations with other derivative rules

Examples to practice multiple derivative rules. In each one, decide on the outermost structure first.

1. If \(f(x) = x^3 e^{5x}\), find \(f'(x)\).

Show solution
  1. Structurea product! \(x^3\) times \(e^{5x}\)
  2. Product rule\(f'(x) = (x^3)'e^{5x} + x^3\left(e^{5x}\right)'\)
  3. Chain inside\(\left(e^{\square}\right)' = e^{\square} \cdot (\square)'\), so \(\left(e^{5x}\right)' = e^{5x} \cdot 5\): \[f'(x) = 3x^2 e^{5x} + x^3 e^{5x} \cdot 5 = (3x^2 + 5x^3)e^{5x}\]

Answer(3x² + 5x³)e⁵ˣ, or (3 + 5x)x²e⁵ˣ

2. Find the derivative of \(f(x) = \dfrac{\log(4 - 3x)}{x + 2}\).

Show solution
  1. Structurethis is a quotient
  2. Quotient rule\[f'(x) = \frac{\left(\log(4-3x)\right)'(x+2) - \log(4-3x)\,(x+2)'}{(x+2)^2}\]
  3. Chain inside\((\log \square)' = \dfrac{1}{\square \ln 10} \cdot (\square)'\) with \(\square = 4 - 3x\) and \((\square)' = -3\): \[f'(x) = \frac{\dfrac{1}{(4-3x)\ln 10} \cdot (-3)(x+2) - \log(4-3x) \cdot 1}{(x+2)^2}\]

Answerf′(x) = [−3(x + 2)/((4 − 3x) ln 10) − log(4 − 3x)] / (x + 2)²

3. Find the derivative of \(y = \left(2x^3 - 8x\right)^{-12}\).

Show solution
  1. Power chainthe inner function is \(2x^3 - 8x\): \[y' = (-12)\left(2x^3 - 8x\right)^{-13} \cdot \left(2x^3 - 8x\right)' = (-12)\left(2x^3 - 8x\right)^{-13}\left(6x^2 - 8\right)\]

Answer−12(2x³ − 8x)⁻¹³(6x² − 8)

4. Find the derivative of \(f(x) = 5^{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}\).

Show solution
  1. Outer\(\left(5^{\square}\right)' = 5^{\square} \cdot \ln 5 \cdot (\square)'\), with inner \(\square = \sqrt{x^2 + 1}\): \[f'(x) = 5^{\sqrt{x^2+1}} \cdot \ln 5 \cdot \left(\sqrt{x^2+1}\right)'\]
  2. 2nd chain rulethe inner function is itself a composition: \(\left(\sqrt{x^2+1}\right)' = \frac12 (x^2+1)^{-1/2} \cdot (2x)\), so \[f'(x) = 5^{\sqrt{x^2+1}} \cdot \ln 5 \cdot \frac12 (x^2+1)^{-1/2} \cdot (2x)\]
  3. Tidy\(\frac12 \cdot 2x = x\), so this is also \(\dfrac{x \ln 5 \cdot 5^{\sqrt{x^2+1}}}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}\)

Answerf′(x) = x ln 5 · 5^√(x² + 1) / √(x² + 1)

5. Find the derivative of \(y = \dfrac{1}{(3 + 5x)^3}\).

Show solution
  1. Rewriteno quotient rule needed: \(y = (3 + 5x)^{-3}\)
  2. Power chain\(y' = -3(3 + 5x)^{-4} \cdot (3 + 5x)' = -3(3 + 5x)^{-4} \cdot 5 = -\dfrac{15}{(3 + 5x)^4}\)

Answery′ = −15/(3 + 5x)⁴

Algebra firstProperties of logarithms can help

When the expression inside a logarithm is a product, quotient, or power, it is best to use the properties of logarithms before taking the derivative.

  1. Step 1, algebra: simplify the logarithm.
  2. Step 2, calculus: find its derivative.

Properties of logarithms

  • \(\ln\dfrac{A}{B} = \ln A - \ln B\)
  • \(\ln(A \cdot B) = \ln A + \ln B\)
  • \(\ln(A^r) = r\ln A\)

Find the derivative of \(y = \ln\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{x}\,(9 - x)^3}{(5x + 3)^2}\right)\).

Show solution
  1. 1. Expandquotient first, then product: \[y = \ln\left(\sqrt{x}\,(9-x)^3\right) - \ln\left((5x+3)^2\right) = \ln\sqrt{x} + \ln\left((9-x)^3\right) - \ln\left((5x+3)^2\right)\]
  2. 1. Powers\[y = \frac12 \ln x + 3\ln(9 - x) - 2\ln(5x + 3)\]
  3. 2. Derivativelogarithmic chain rule on each term: \[y' = \frac12 \cdot \frac1x + 3 \cdot \frac{1}{9-x} \cdot (9-x)' - 2 \cdot \frac{1}{5x+3} \cdot (5x+3)'\]
  4. Simplifywith \((9 - x)' = -1\) and \((5x + 3)' = 5\): \[y' = \frac{1}{2x} - \frac{3}{9-x} - \frac{10}{5x+3}\]

Answery′ = 1/(2x) − 3/(9 − x) − 10/(5x + 3)

Trying to do this directly, without algebraic preparation, would produce a mess: a logarithmic chain rule wrapped around a quotient rule wrapped around a product rule.
