The product rule

Section 10.3, part 1. The derivative distributes over sums but not over products. For a product of two functions, the derivative falls on each factor in turn, and the two results are added.

RecallSums yes, products no

Recall that the derivative can be distributed over \(+\) and \(-\) signs:

\[(f + g)' = f' + g' \qquad\qquad (f - g)' = f' - g'\]

Find \(\left(x^5 - x^3 + \dfrac{5}{x}\right)'\).

Show solution
  1. Rewrite\(\dfrac{5}{x} = 5x^{-1}\)
  2. Distribute\[(x^5)' - (x^3)' + 5(x^{-1})' = 5x^4 - 3x^2 + 5(-1)x^{-2} = 5x^4 - 3x^2 - \frac{5}{x^2}\]

Answer5x⁴ − 3x² − 5/x²

But the derivative does not distribute over products.

Consider \(f(x) = x^5\) and \(g(x) = x^3\). Find the derivative of the product \(fg\) and compare it to \(f'g'\).

Show solution
  1. (fg)′the product is \(x^5 \cdot x^3 = x^8\), so its derivative is \(8x^7\)
  2. f′g′\((x^5)' \cdot (x^3)' = 5x^4 \cdot 3x^2 = 15x^6\)

Answer(fg)′ = 8x⁷, but f′g′ = 15x⁶. Not the same.

The wrong step

Writing \((x^5 \cdot x^3)' = (x^5)' \cdot (x^3)'\) is incorrect: \((f \cdot g)' \ne f' \cdot g'\). And this example was easy only because \(x^5 \cdot x^3\) simplifies to \(x^8\). We need to find \((f \cdot g)'\) even when the product does not simplify like that.

RuleThe product rule

The product rule applies when we have a function that is a product of two other functions. The textbook calls these two factors \(F\) and \(S\), F for “first” and S for “second.”

Worth memorizing

  • \[(FS)' = F'S + FS'\]
  • In words: first · (derivative of second) + second · (derivative of first).

It could be written in other orders, like \((FS)' = FS' + SF'\), since the order of terms does not matter for addition or multiplication.

A way to remember this without a formula is to focus on the process: “the derivative falls on the first factor only, then on the second one only.”

Check that the product rule gives the right answer for \(x^5 \cdot x^3\).

Show solution
  1. Product rule\((x^5 \cdot x^3)' = (x^5)'x^3 + x^5(x^3)'\)
  2. Compute\(= 5x^4 \cdot x^3 + x^5 \cdot 3x^2 = 5x^7 + 3x^7 = 8x^7\)

Answer8x⁷, which matches (x⁸)′

IntuitionWhy it works

Think of \(F(x)\) as the quantity sold and \(S(x)\) as the price per unit. Then the product \(F(x)S(x)\) is the total revenue.

If both \(F\) and \(S\) are increasing, the revenue will grow for two reasons:

  • the greater sales volume contributes \(F'S\);
  • the higher price contributes \(FS'\).

PracticeWhen a factor is unknown

The product rule works even when we don’t know a formula for one of the factors.

Suppose that \(f(x) = x^2 h(x)\), where \(h\) is some function we don’t know. Find \(f'(x)\).

Show solution
  1. Factorsthe first factor is \(x^2\) and the second is \(h(x)\)
  2. Product rule\((x^2 \cdot h(x))' = (x^2)' \cdot h(x) + x^2 \cdot h'(x) = 2x \cdot h(x) + x^2 \cdot h'(x)\)

Answerf′(x) = 2x·h(x) + x²·h′(x)

Let’s try another: \(f(x) = \sqrt{x}\, g(x)\). Find \(f'(x)\).

Show solution
  1. Product rule\(f'(x) = (\sqrt{x})'\, g(x) + \sqrt{x}\, (g(x))'\)
  2. Compute\(= \dfrac12 x^{-1/2}\, g(x) + \sqrt{x}\, g'(x)\)

Answerf′(x) = (1/2)x⁻¹∕²g(x) + √x·g′(x)

JudgmentWhen (not) to use the product rule

Not every derivative of a product needs the product rule. Here are two situations when it is unnecessary.

1. A constant factor can simply be pulled out: find \((7x^5)'\).    2. Two powers with the same base can be combined: find \((x^7\sqrt{x})'\).

Show solution
  1. 1\((7x^5)' = 7(x^5)' = 7 \cdot 5x^4 = 35x^4\)
  2. 2\(x^7 \cdot x^{1/2} = x^{7 + \frac12} = x^{7.5}\), so \((x^{7.5})' = 7.5x^{6.5}\)

Answer35x⁴  ·  7.5x⁶·⁵

In other cases, when we need the derivative of a product, we use the product rule even if the product could be expanded. For the next function, would you rather find \(f'(t)\) with the product rule, or multiply out all the terms first?

Find \(f'(t)\) for \(f(t) = (t^2 + 3t - 7)(2t^3 - 4)\).

Show solution
  1. Product rule\[f'(t) = (t^2 + 3t - 7)'(2t^3 - 4) + (t^2 + 3t - 7)(2t^3 - 4)'\]
  2. Compute\[= (2t + 3)(2t^3 - 4) + (t^2 + 3t - 7)(6t^2)\]

Answerf′(t) = (2t + 3)(2t³ − 4) + (t² + 3t − 7)(6t²)

And then there are examples where we absolutely cannot avoid the product rule: products with an exponential or logarithmic function. Recall from Friday that \((\ln x)' = \dfrac{1}{x}\) and \((e^x)' = e^x\).

(a) Find the derivative of \(y = x^5 \ln x\).    (b) Find the derivative of \(g(w) = e^w \sqrt{w}\).

Show solution
  1. (a)first factor \(x^5\), second factor \(\ln x\): \[y' = (x^5)'\ln x + x^5(\ln x)' = 5x^4 \ln x + x^5 \cdot \frac{1}{x} = 5x^4 \ln x + x^4\]
  2. (b)first factor \(e^w\), second factor \(\sqrt{w} = w^{1/2}\): \[g'(w) = (e^w)'\sqrt{w} + e^w(\sqrt{w})' = e^w\sqrt{w} + e^w\left(\frac12\right)w^{-1/2}\]

Answer(a) 5x⁴ ln x + x⁴   (b) eʷ√w + (1/2)eʷw⁻¹∕²

Two small details

In (a), \(x^5 \cdot \frac{1}{x} = x^4\): one power of \(x\) cancels. In (b), \((w^{1/2})' = \frac12 w^{-1/2}\), since \(\frac12 - 1 = -\frac12\).

Big pictureWhich rule to use?

The key to finding derivatives is the correct identification of a given function.

Rules so far
The function isIts derivative is
Sum or difference: \((\square) \pm (\square)\)\((\square)' \pm (\square)'\)
Something with a constant factor, like \(7 \cdot (\square)\)\(7 \cdot (\square)'\)
Just a constant, like \(7\) or \(\ln 7\) or \(e^7\) or \(\sqrt{7}\)\(0\)
Power of the variable: \(x^a\)\(a x^{a-1}\)
Product: \((\square) \cdot (\blacksquare)\)\((\square)' \cdot (\blacksquare) + (\square) \cdot (\blacksquare)'\)

In short

  • Sum or difference: distribute the derivative.
  • Constant factor: pull it out of the derivative.
  • Power of \(x\), or something that can be written as a power of \(x\): \((x^a)' = ax^{a-1}\).
  • Product: use \(F'S + FS'\).

We’ll deal with quotients next time.

Find \(\left(x^7 \log_5 x - 3 \cdot 10^x\right)'\).

Show solution
  1. Structurea difference, so distribute: \((x^7 \log_5 x)' - (3 \cdot 10^x)'\)
  2. Rulesproduct rule on the first term, constant factor on the second: \[(x^7)'\log_5 x + x^7(\log_5 x)' - 3(10^x)'\]
  3. Computewith \((\log_5 x)' = \dfrac{1}{x\ln 5}\) and \((10^x)' = 10^x \ln 10\): \[7x^6 \log_5 x + x^7 \cdot \frac{1}{x\ln 5} - 3 \cdot 10^x \ln 10\]

Answer7x⁶ log₅ x + x⁷/(x ln 5) − 3·10ˣ ln 10

The middle term simplifies to \(\dfrac{x^6}{\ln 5}\), if you like.

One has to recognize the overall structure of a formula without getting lost in the details. Some WeBWorK problems help by hiding the details.

1. Given that \(f(x) = x^4 h(x)\), where \(h(2) = 3\) and \(h'(2) = 6\), find \(f'(2)\).

2. Suppose \(H(x) = f(x)g(x)\), where \(g(4) = -9\), \(g'(4) = -2\), \(f(4) = 3\), \(f'(4) = -1\). Find \(H'(4)\).

Show solution
  1. 1. Rule\(f'(x) = (x^4)'h(x) + x^4 h'(x) = 4x^3 h(x) + x^4 h'(x)\)
  2. 1. Plug in 2\(f'(2) = 4 \cdot 2^3 \cdot h(2) + 2^4 \cdot h'(2) = 4 \cdot 8 \cdot 3 + 16 \cdot 6 = 96 + 96 = 192\)
  3. 2. Rule\(H'(x) = f'(x)g(x) + f(x)g'(x)\)
  4. 2. Plug in 4\(H'(4) = f'(4)g(4) + f(4)g'(4) = (-1)(-9) + 3(-2) = 9 - 6 = 3\)

Answerf′(2) = 192  ·  H′(4) = 3

Differentiate first, then plug in

Apply the product rule to the formula with \(x\) in it, and only then substitute the number. Plugging in first turns \(f(2)\) into a single number, \(2^4 \cdot 3 = 48\), whose derivative is 0, which is not the answer.
