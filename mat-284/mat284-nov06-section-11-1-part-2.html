Warm-upFinding derivatives

Rules in play

  • \((\square \cdot \blacksquare)' = (\square)' \cdot \blacksquare + \square \cdot (\blacksquare)'\)
  • \(\left(e^{\square}\right)' = e^{\square} \cdot (\square)'\)
  • \((\ln \square)' = \dfrac{1}{\square} \cdot (\square)'\), or \(\dfrac{(\square)'}{\square}\)

1. Find \(y'\) given \(y = t^2 e^{-3t}\).

Show solution
  1. Product rule\(y' = (t^2)'e^{-3t} + t^2\left(e^{-3t}\right)'\)
  2. Chain insidethe inner function of \(e^{-3t}\) is \(-3t\): \[y' = 2t\,e^{-3t} + t^2 e^{-3t} \cdot (-3t)' = 2t\,e^{-3t} + t^2 e^{-3t} \cdot (-3)\]

Answery′ = 2te⁻³ᵗ − 3t²e⁻³ᵗ

2. Find the derivative of \(y = \ln(1 - x^3)\).

Show solution
  1. Log chainthe inner function is \(1 - x^3\): \[y' = \frac{1}{1 - x^3} \cdot (1 - x^3)' = \frac{1}{1 - x^3} \cdot (-3x^2) = \frac{-3x^2}{1 - x^3}\]

Answer−3x²/(1 − x³), or 3x²/(x³ − 1)

3. Find \(f'(x)\) where \(f(x) = \log\left(\dfrac{(3x - 2)^2}{x^4 + 1}\right)\).

Show solution

Should we use \((\log_{10} \square)' = \dfrac{1}{\square \ln 10} \cdot (\square)'\) right away? Better to expand the logarithm first.

  1. Quotient\(\log\dfrac{A}{B} = \log A - \log B\): \(f(x) = \log\left((3x - 2)^2\right) - \log(x^4 + 1)\)
  2. Power\(\log(A^r) = r\log A\): \(f(x) = 2\log(3x - 2) - \log(x^4 + 1)\), a simpler way of writing \(f(x)\)
  3. Now f′the inner functions are \(3x - 2\) and \(x^4 + 1\): \[f'(x) = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{(3x-2)\ln 10} \cdot \underbrace{(3x-2)'}_{3} - \frac{1}{(x^4+1)\ln 10} \cdot \underbrace{(x^4+1)'}_{4x^3}\]
  4. Simplify\[f'(x) = \frac{6}{(3x-2)\ln 10} - \frac{4x^3}{(x^4+1)\ln 10}\]

Answerf′(x) = 6/((3x − 2) ln 10) − 4x³/((x⁴ + 1) ln 10)

RecapMain points from last time

Partition numbers

Partition numbers for \(f'\) are where \(f' = 0\), or \(f'\) is undefined, or \(f\) is undefined.

Why we need them: they divide the number line into intervals where \(f\) is increasing or decreasing. We then find the sign of \(f'\) in each interval by plugging in some test number.

Critical numbers

Critical numbers are the partition numbers of the first two kinds: \(f' = 0\) or \(f'\) is undefined (with \(f\) defined).

Why we need them: they are candidates for local extrema. To find what kind of extremum we have, consider the signs of \(f'\) to the left and to the right:

  • The sign change \((+\ -)\) indicates a local maximum.
  • The sign change \((-\ +)\) indicates a local minimum.
  • No sign change \((+\ +\) or \(-\ -)\) indicates it’s not an extremum at all.

Each example below asks where the function is (a) increasing, (b) decreasing, and (c) has local maxima and minima. All three questions are answered by one sign chart for \(f'\).

Example 1A cubic

For the function \(f(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 - 36x\), determine where it is (a) increasing; (b) decreasing; (c) has local maxima and minima.

Show solution
  1. f′ first\(f'(x) = 2 \cdot 3x^2 - 3 \cdot 2x - 36 = 6x^2 - 6x - 36 = 6(x^2 - x - 6) = 6(x - 3)(x + 2)\)
  2. Partition\(f' = 0\) when \(x = 3\) or \(x = -2\); \(f'\) is never undefined
  3. Test −3\(f'(-3) = 6(-3 - 3)(-3 + 2) = 6(-6)(-1) > 0\)
  4. Test 0\(f'(0) = 6(-3)(2) < 0\)
  5. Test 4\(f'(4) = 6(4 - 3)(4 + 2) = 6 \cdot 1 \cdot 6 > 0\)
  6. Values\(f(-2) = -16 - 12 + 72 = 44\);   \(f(3) = 54 - 27 - 108 = -81\)

Answer(a) increasing on (−∞, −2) and (3, ∞)   (b) decreasing on (−2, 3)   (c) local max at x = −2, local min at x = 3

Example 2A quotient

Same questions for \(f(x) = \dfrac{x^2}{3 - x}\).

Show solution
  1. Quotient rule\[f'(x) = \frac{(x^2)'(3 - x) - x^2(3 - x)'}{(3 - x)^2} = \frac{2x(3 - x) - x^2(-1)}{(3 - x)^2}\]
  2. Simplifysimplify, so that we can solve \(f' = 0\): \[f'(x) = \frac{6x - 2x^2 + x^2}{(3 - x)^2} = \frac{6x - x^2}{(3 - x)^2} = \frac{x(6 - x)}{(3 - x)^2}\]
  3. f′ = 0numerator = 0: \(x(6 - x) = 0\), so \(x = 0\) or \(x = 6\)
  4. Undefineddenominator = 0: \((3 - x)^2 = 0\), so \(x = 3\). There \(f\) is undefined too.
  5. Test\(f'(-1) = \frac{(-1)(7)}{+} < 0\);   \(f'(1) = \frac{1(6-1)}{+} > 0\);   \(f'(4) = \frac{4(6-4)}{+} > 0\);   \(f'(7) = \frac{7(6-7)}{+} < 0\). The denominator is a square, so it is always positive.
  6. Values\(f(0) = 0\);   \(f(6) = \dfrac{36}{-3} = -12\)

Answer(a) increasing on (0, 3) and (3, 6)   (b) decreasing on (−∞, 0) and (6, ∞)   (c) local max at x = 6, local min at x = 0

Two intervals, not one

\(f\) is increasing on \((0, 3)\) and on \((3, 6)\), but not on \((0, 6)\): the function is undefined at 3, and it jumps from \(+\infty\) down to \(-\infty\) there. That is also why the local maximum value, \(-12\), is lower than the local minimum value, 0.

Example 3A product

Same questions for \(f(x) = x^3 e^x\).

Show solution
  1. Product rule\(f'(x) = (x^3)'e^x + x^3(e^x)' = 3x^2 e^x + x^3 e^x = (3x^2 + x^3)e^x = x^2(3 + x)e^x\)
  2. f′ = 0\(x^2 \cdot (3 + x) \cdot e^x = 0\): either \(x^2 = 0\), so \(x = 0\); or \(3 + x = 0\), so \(x = -3\); or \(e^x = 0\), which never happens, since \(e^x\) is always \(> 0\)
  3. Test\(f'(-4) = 16(-1)e^{-4} < 0\);   \(f'(-1) = 1 \cdot 2 \cdot e^{-1} > 0\);   \(f'(1) = 1 \cdot 4 \cdot e > 0\)
  4. Classify\(x = -3\): \((-\ +)\), a local min, with \(f(-3) = -27e^{-3} \approx -1.34\).   \(x = 0\): \((+\ +)\), no extremum.

Answer(a) increasing on (−3, 0) and (0, ∞)   (b) decreasing on (−∞, −3)   (c) local max: none; local min at x = −3

A critical number that is not an extremum

At \(x = 0\), \(f'(0) = 0\), so the tangent line is horizontal, but \(f'\) is positive on both sides: the graph keeps rising through it. The factor \(x^2\) is the reason, because a square does not change sign.
