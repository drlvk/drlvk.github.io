MethodRules for solving a linear inequality

Last time we solved linear equations such as \(5x + 3 = 7(x - 3)\). A linear inequality looks exactly the same, except that in place of \(=\) it carries one of four signs.

The four signs

  • \(>\)   greater than
  • \(<\)   less than
  • \(\ge\)   greater than or equal (equality allowed)
  • \(\le\)   less than or equal (equality allowed)

You solve one the same way as an equation: gather all the unknown terms on one side. The moves available to you are these.

  • Add or subtract the same quantity on both sides.
  • Multiply or divide both sides by the same number — but if that number is negative, the inequality sign changes direction.

The one rule that differs from equations

Start from \(3 < 5\), which is true.

Multiply both sides by \(4\), a positive number: \(12 < 20\). The sign stays \(<\).

Multiply both sides by \(-2\): the correct statement is \(-6 > -10\), not “\(-6 < -10\).” Multiplying by a negative flips the sign.

Worked exampleTwo inequalities

Solve \(-20x < 100\) and express the answer as an interval.

Show solution
  1. Divide by −20\[\frac{-20x}{-20} \;\;\boldsymbol{>}\;\; \frac{100}{-20}\]
  2. Flip\(-20\) is negative, so \(<\) becomes \(>\)
  3. Simplify\[x > -5\]
  4. IntervalEverything to the right of \(-5\), endpoint excluded: \((-5, \infty)\)

Answer(−5, ∞)

Check one value: \(x = 0\) gives \(0 < 100\). ✓ And \(x = -10\) gives \(200 < 100\), which is false — so the solution really is the right-hand side.

Solve \(10(3x - 40) \ge 50 + 10x\) and express the answer as an interval.

Show solution
  1. Distribute\[30x - 400 \ge 50 + 10x\]
  2. −10x, +400\[30x - 10x \ge 50 + 400\]
  3. Combine\[20x \ge 450\]
  4. Divide by 20\[x \ge \frac{450}{20} = 22.5\]
  5. Interval\(20\) is positive, so the sign does not move. Endpoint included: \([22.5, \infty)\)

Answer[22.5, ∞)

Where the bracket comes from

Nothing about the interval is a separate decision. A strict sign gives a parenthesis, a non-strict sign gives a square bracket, and infinity always gets a parenthesis. Read the bracket straight off the final inequality.

MethodCompound inequalities

A double or compound inequality packs two inequalities into one line. For example

\[-2 \le 3x + 1 < 10\]

combines two statements about \(3x + 1\): that it is at least \(-2\), and that it is below \(10\). You can solve them one at a time, or handle both at once by doing the same thing to all three parts.

Solve \(-2 \le 3x + 1 < 10\) and express the answer as an interval.

Show solution
  1. Subtract 1\[-2 - 1 \le 3x + 1 - 1 < 10 - 1\]
  2. Simplify\[-3 \le 3x < 9\]
  3. Divide all by 3\[-1 \le x < 3\]
  4. Interval\(-1\) included, \(3\) excluded: \([-1, 3)\)

Answer[−1, 3)

Poll question from class

Solve \(3 < 5 - 2x \le 10\) and express the answer as an interval.

Show solution
  1. Subtract 5\[3 - 5 < -2x \le 10 - 5\]
  2. Simplify\[-2 < -2x \le 5\]
  3. Divide by −2both signs flip: \[1 > x \ge -2.5\]
  4. Rewriteread it left to right, smallest first: \[-2.5 \le x < 1\]
  5. Interval\([-2.5,\ 1)\)

Answer[−2.5, 1)

Watch out

When you divide a compound inequality by a negative number, both signs flip and the whole line reverses. After that, rewrite it with the smaller number on the left before reading off the interval — otherwise it is easy to write \((1, -2.5]\), which describes nothing.

Notice also which endpoint kept which bracket. The \(-2.5\) came from the \(\le\), so it stays included; the \(1\) came from the \(<\), so it stays excluded. Reversing the line does not change that.

MethodTranslating word problems to math

Many word problems in this course become a linear equation or inequality that you then solve. The translation step is usually the hard part, and it is where most of the points are.

This being business calculus, the problems tend to involve revenue, cost, and profit. Four relations connect them.

Worth memorizing

  • profit \(=\) (total revenue) \(-\) (total cost)
  • (total revenue) \(=\) (price per unit) \(\cdot\) (number of units sold)
  • (total cost) \(=\) (fixed cost) \(+\) (variable cost)
  • (variable cost) \(=\) (cost per unit) \(\cdot\) (number of units produced)

Fill in the blanks.

To break even means Revenue ___ Total Cost.

To make a profit means Revenue ___ Total Cost.

Show solution
  1. Break evenRevenue \(=\) Total Cost (profit is exactly zero)
  2. Make a profitRevenue \(>\) Total Cost (profit is strictly greater than zero)

Answer= for break even, > for profit

So the recipe is: identify the business terms in the problem, recall the relation between them, then replace each term with a number, a formula, or a letter standing for an unknown.

The textbook writes \(p\) for price and \(x\) for the number of units, though \(x\) also gets used for other unknowns. Whatever letter you pick, say in words what it stands for before you use it.

Worked exampleBuilding a profit formula

Fixed cost is $100,000, variable cost is $15 per unit, and the selling price is $25. Express the profit in terms of \(x\), the number of units sold.

Show solution
  1. Revenue\[\text{revenue} = 25x\]
  2. Total cost\[\text{total cost} = 100000 + 15x\]
  3. Profit\[\text{profit} = 25x - (100000 + 15x)\]
  4. Expand\[= 25x - 100000 - 15x\]
  5. Combine\[\text{profit} = 10x - 100000\]

Formulaprofit = 10x − 100000

The minus sign in front of the parenthesis hits both terms inside it. Dropping it on the \(15x\) is the most common error here.

How many units must be sold to reach a profit of $50,000?

Show solution
  1. Goal\[\text{profit} = 50000\]
  2. Substituteput the formula for profit in: \[10x - 100000 = 50000\]
  3. Add 100000\[10x = 150000\]
  4. Divide by 10\[x = 15000\]

Answer15,000 units

MethodInequalities in word problems

You get an inequality rather than an equation when the stated goal is to make a profit — that is, profit \(> 0\) — or when the words at most, at least, more, or less appear.

Strict vs. non-strict, when the answer must be a whole number

The smallest \(x\) satisfying \(5x \ge 150\) is \(x = 30\).

But if the requirement is \(5x > 150\), then \(x > 30\), and 30 itself is not allowed. If \(x\) must be a whole number, the smallest one is \(31\).

Your business has fixed cost $100,000, variable cost $15 per unit, and each unit sells for $25.

a) How many units — a whole number — must be produced and sold to make a profit?

Show solution
  1. Recallfrom the previous section, \(\text{profit} = 10x - 100000\)
  2. Goal“make a profit” means \(\text{profit} > 0\)
  3. Inequality\[10x - 100000 > 0\]
  4. Add 100000\[10x > 100000\]
  5. Divide by 10\[x > 10000\]
  6. Whole number\(x = 10000\) gives profit exactly \(0\), which is breaking even, not a profit

Answer10,001 units

b) How many units must be produced and sold for the profit to be at least $20,000?

Show solution
  1. Goal“at least” is non-strict: \(\text{profit} \ge 20000\)
  2. Inequality\[10x - 100000 \ge 20000\]
  3. Add 100000\[10x \ge 120000\]
  4. Divide by 10\[x \ge 12000\]
  5. Whole number\(12000\) is allowed here, because \(\ge\) includes equality

Answer12,000 units

Compare (a) and (b)

The algebra is identical; only the sign differs. In (a) the boundary value 10,000 is excluded, so the answer is 10,001. In (b) the boundary value 12,000 is included, so the answer is 12,000. Reading “make a profit” as \(\ge\) would cost you the point.
