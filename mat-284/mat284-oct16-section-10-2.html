Warm-up · Section 9.7From demand to marginal profit

For a new toaster model, a company expects weekly demand of 100 toasters at $25 each and 200 toasters at $20 each. Fixed weekly costs are $4000, and variable costs are $9 per toaster.

  1. Assume that price \(p\) and demand \(x\) have a linear relationship. Express \(p\) as a function of \(x\).
  2. Find the revenue function.
  3. Find the cost function.
  4. Find the profit function.
  5. Find the marginal profit function.
Show solution
Data for the price-demand line
Weekly demand \(x\) (toasters)Price \(p\) (dollars per toaster)
10025
20020
  1. A. SlopeTreat \(x\) as the input and \(p\) as the output: \[\frac{20-25}{200-100}=\frac{-5}{100}=-0.05.\]
  2. PriceUse point-slope form through \((100,25)\): \[\begin{aligned} p-25&=-0.05(x-100),\\ p&=25-0.05x+5\\ &=30-0.05x. \end{aligned}\]
  3. B. RevenueRevenue is price times quantity: \[R(x)=p(x)\,x=(30-0.05x)x=30x-0.05x^2.\]
  4. C. CostAdd fixed and variable costs: \[C(x)=4000+9x.\]
  5. D. ProfitSubtract the entire cost function: \[\begin{aligned} P(x)&=R(x)-C(x)\\ &=30x-0.05x^2-(4000+9x)\\ &=21x-0.05x^2-4000. \end{aligned}\]
  6. E. MarginalDifferentiate total profit: \[P'(x)=21-0.05\cdot 2x-0=21-0.1x.\]

Marginal profit\(P'(x)=21-0.1x\)

Marginal means derivative. Find total profit first, then differentiate it.

Section 10.2Where is the variable?

Before choosing a derivative rule, distinguish these two kinds of functions.

Power functions versus exponential functions
TypeVariable and constantExamples
PowerThe base is \(x\); the exponent is constant.\(x^3,\quad x^{-1/2}\)
ExponentialThe base is constant; the exponent is \(x\).\(3^x,\quad e^x\)

The power rule \((x^a)'=ax^{a-1}\) applies to a constant exponent \(a\). It does not give the derivative of \(3^x\): the expression \(x3^{x-1}\) is not the answer.

Classify each function as a power function or an exponential function:

\[\sqrt{x},\qquad 2^x,\qquad x^{3/2},\qquad (\sqrt{2})^x,\qquad e^x.\]
Show classification
  1. Square root\(\sqrt{x}=x^{1/2}\) is a power function: the exponent \(1/2\) is constant.
  2. Base 2\(2^x\) is an exponential function: \(x\) is in the exponent.
  3. Power 3/2\(x^{3/2}\) is a power function: the exponent \(3/2\) is constant.
  4. Base √2\((\sqrt{2})^x\) is an exponential function: its base \(\sqrt{2}\) is constant.
  5. Base e\(e^x\) is an exponential function: its base \(e\) is constant.

Exponential functionsBase e gives the simplest derivative

Recall the base of the natural logarithm, \(e=2.71828\ldots\). It often appears as the base of an exponential function as well; \(e^x\) has its own button on a calculator.

The base \(e\) is especially convenient for calculus because its exponential function is its own derivative:

Derivative of the natural exponential

\[(e^x)'=e^x.\]

The slope of the graph at any \(x\) is the same as its height \(e^x\).

No other positive base has this property. The explanation is beyond this course; here we use the rule.

Find the tangent line to \(y=e^x\) at the point \((0,1)\).

Show solution
  1. Derivative\(y'=(e^x)'=e^x\).
  2. Slope at 0\(e^0=1\).
  3. Tangent lineUse the point \((0,1)\) and slope 1: \[y-1=1(x-0).\]

Answer\(y=x+1\)

With equal scales on the axes, this tangent makes a \(45^\circ\) angle with the positive \(x\)-axis. Its slope of 1 at the \(y\)-intercept distinguishes \(e^x\) from exponentials with other bases.

Other exponential basesMultiply by the logarithm of the base

Although \(e\) is the most common base in calculus, we also encounter functions such as \(2^x\) and \(1.05^x\). Their derivative has one extra constant factor:

Exponential derivative rule

\[(b^x)'=b^x\ln b,\qquad b>0.\]

The base \(b\) is constant. For \(b=e\), the extra factor is \(\ln e=1\), giving the rule above.

For example,

\[(10^x)'=10^x\ln 10,\qquad \ln 10\approx 2.302585093.\]

Given \(f(x)=2^x\):

  1. Find \(f'(3)\).
  2. Find the tangent line to the graph at \((3,8)\).
Show solution
  1. Differentiate\(f'(x)=2^x\ln 2\).
  2. Evaluate\(f'(3)=2^3\ln 2=8\ln 2\).
  3. Point and slopeUse the point \((3,8)\) and slope \(f'(3)=8\ln 2\): \[y-8=(8\ln 2)(x-3).\]

Derivative at 3\(f'(3)=8\ln 2\)

Tangent line\(y=8+(8\ln 2)(x-3)\)

Differentiate first; substitute second. The slope of a tangent line is the derivative evaluated at the given \(x\)-coordinate.

Logarithmic functionsThe natural logarithm

The logarithm with base \(e\) has its own name and notation: the natural logarithm, written \(\ln x\). Think of the letters as “logarithm” and “natural.” Its derivative is particularly simple:

Derivative of the natural logarithm

\[(\ln x)'=\frac{1}{x},\qquad x>0.\]

The derivation of this formula is beyond this course. We will use it to compute slopes and tangent lines.

Find the tangent line to \(y=\ln x\) at the point \((1,0)\).

Show solution
  1. Derivative\(\displaystyle y'=\frac{1}{x}\).
  2. Slope at 1\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{1}=1\).
  3. Tangent lineUse the point \((1,0)\) and slope 1: \[y-0=1(x-1).\]

Answer\(y=x-1\)

With equal scales on the axes, the tangent to \(\ln x\) at its \(x\)-intercept also makes a \(45^\circ\) angle with the positive \(x\)-axis. Among logarithmic functions, base \(e\) is the one with slope 1 at \((1,0)\).

Algebra before calculusExpand logarithms first

Sometimes the properties of logarithms let us rewrite a function before taking its derivative.

Logarithm properties

  • \(\ln(AB)=\ln A+\ln B\)
  • \(\ln(A/B)=\ln A-\ln B\)
  • \(\ln(A^r)=r\ln A\)
  1. 1. AlgebraSimplify the expression and expand the logarithm.
  2. 2. CalculusDifferentiate the resulting sum, remembering that constants have derivative zero.

Find the derivative of \(y=\ln(5x^3)\).

Show solution
  1. Expand\(\displaystyle y=\ln 5+\ln(x^3)=\ln 5+3\ln x\).
  2. Differentiate\(\displaystyle y'=(\ln 5)'+(3\ln x)'=0+3\cdot\frac1x\).

Answer\(\displaystyle y'=\frac{3}{x}\)

Common mistake

The expression \(\displaystyle \frac15+\frac{1}{3x}\) is incorrect.

  • \((\ln 5)'=0\), because \(\ln 5\) is a constant: there is no \(x\) in it.
  • \(\displaystyle (3\ln x)'=3(\ln x)'=\frac3x\), not \(\displaystyle \frac{1}{3x}\).

Next, find \(f'(x)\) for \[f(x)=3\ln\left(\frac{4x^2}{\sqrt{x}}\right).\]

Show solution
  1. Combine powers\(\displaystyle \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{x}}=\frac{x^2}{x^{1/2}}=x^{2-1/2}=x^{3/2}\).
  2. Expand \[\begin{aligned} f(x)&=3\ln(4x^{3/2})\\ &=3\left(\ln 4+\ln(x^{3/2})\right)\\ &=3\left(\ln 4+\frac32\ln x\right)\\ &=3\ln 4+\frac92\ln x. \end{aligned}\]
  3. Differentiate\(\displaystyle f'(x)=0+\frac92\cdot\frac1x=\frac{9}{2x}\).

Answer\(\displaystyle f'(x)=\frac{9}{2x}\)

Other logarithmic basesDivide by the logarithm of the base

To differentiate a logarithm with another base \(b\), start with the change-of-base formula:

\[\log_b x=\frac{\ln x}{\ln b}.\]

The denominator is constant: there is no \(x\) in \(\ln b\). Therefore,

\[(\log_b x)'=\frac{1}{\ln b}(\ln x)'=\frac{1}{x\ln b}.\]

Compare the two rules

  • \(\displaystyle (b^x)'=b^x\ln b\): multiply by the logarithm of the base.
  • \(\displaystyle (\log_b x)'=\frac{1}{x\ln b}\): divide by the logarithm of the base.

For bases \(b>1\), increasing the base makes \(\ln b\) larger. The factor \(\ln b\) increases the exponential’s relative rate of change, while the factor \(1/\ln b\) decreases the logarithm’s slope at a fixed positive \(x\).

Here \(\log x\) means \(\log_{10}x\), the common logarithm.

  1. Find the derivative of \(y=\log x\).
  2. Find the tangent line at \((100,2)\).
Show solution
  1. Derivative\(\displaystyle y'=\frac{1}{x\ln 10}\), equivalently \(\displaystyle \frac{1}{(\ln 10)x}\).
  2. Slope at 100Substitute \(x=100\) into the derivative: \[\frac{1}{100\ln 10}.\]
  3. Tangent lineUse the point \((100,2)\) and this slope: \[y-2=\frac{1}{100\ln 10}(x-100).\]

Derivative\(\displaystyle y'=\frac{1}{x\ln 10}\)

Tangent line\(\displaystyle y-2=\frac{x-100}{100\ln 10}\)
