DefinitionWhat continuity means

Recall that the limit of \(f(x)\) as \(x \to c\) is not the same concept as plugging \(x = c\) into the function. But very often, these two different concepts produce the same value.

Worth memorizing

A function is continuous at a point \(c\) if it has both a value and a limit there, and they are equal:

  • \[\lim_{x\to c} f(x) = f(c)\]

A point at which a function is not continuous is called a point of discontinuity.

That one equation is really three conditions at once: \(f(c)\) must exist, the limit must exist, and the two must agree. Failing any of the three makes \(c\) a point of discontinuity.

On a graph, points of discontinuity are the places where the values of \(f\) change suddenly: a jump, a hole, a point floating off by itself, or a vertical asymptote. A continuous stretch of graph is one you could trace without lifting your pen.

Worked exampleReading it off a graph

Use the graph below to find the points of discontinuity.

Show solution

Look for every place the pen would have to leave the paper, then say which condition fails there.

  1. x = −2the left and right pieces approach different heights, and \(f(-2)\) sits at a third height. The graph is detached from the point at \(x = -2\).
  2. x = 2\(f(2)\) is the filled dot at the bottom, where the right-hand piece starts. The left-hand piece approaches a different height, so \(\lim\limits_{x\to 2^-}f(x) \ne f(2)\).
  3. x = 4\(f(4)\) is not defined, and the one-sided limits are infinite: a vertical asymptote
  4. x = 6again detached from both sides: the two pieces approach different heights, and \(f(6)\) is neither of them
  5. x = 8the graph ends in an open circle, so \(f(8)\) is not defined

Answerx = −2, 2, 4, 6, and 8

Different ways to fail

At \(x = 4\) and \(x = 8\) there is no value at all. At \(x = -2\) and \(x = 6\) there is a value but no limit. At \(x = 2\) the function is continuous from the right, since the right-hand piece starts at the filled dot, but not from the left. All five are discontinuities; they fail the definition for different reasons.

And why not \(x = -4\)? The graph starts there with a filled dot, so \(f(-4)\) exists and the graph is attached to it from the only side the function has. That is different from \(x = 8\), where the endpoint is an open circle and there is no value.

ToolContinuous where defined

Many functions are continuous at every point where they are defined. And note that a function can never be continuous at a point where it is not defined, since continuity requires a value.

Continuous wherever they are defined

  • Polynomials and rational functions
  • Radicals (roots)
  • Exponential functions and logarithms
  • The absolute value function
  • Combinations of everything above

For example, \(\sqrt{e^x + x^3} - \ln x\) is continuous wherever it is defined, which is \(x > 0\): the logarithm requires it.

This list covers almost everything in the course, except piecewise functions, which are treated separately below. So if a function is not piecewise, the two questions

  • where is \(f\) not continuous?
  • where is \(f\) not defined?

have the same answer. Finding discontinuities becomes a domain problem, which you already know how to solve.

Poll question from class

Find all points where \(f(x) = \dfrac{5x - 10}{x^2 - 1}\) is not continuous.

Show solution

It is a rational function, not piecewise, so the question is the same as asking where it is not defined.

  1. Denominator\[x^2 - 1 = 0\]
  2. Solve\(x = 1\) or \(x = -1\)

Answerx = 1 and x = −1

The numerator's zero at \(x = 2\) is irrelevant here. There \(f(2) = 0\), a perfectly good value, and nothing sudden happens.

MethodPiecewise functions

Piecewise functions need careful investigation at the points of transition, where different formulas apply on the two sides. Everywhere else, each piece is a polynomial or similar, and is continuous.

At a point of transition \(c\), find three things

  • the limit from the left, \(\lim\limits_ {x\to c^-}f(x)\)
  • the limit from the right, \(\lim\limits_ {x\to c^+}f(x)\)
  • the function value at the point, \(f(c)\)

If all three are equal, the function is continuous at \(c\). Otherwise it is not.

If \(\lim\limits_ {x\to c^-}f(x) = f(c)\), the graph connects to the point \((c, f(c))\) from the left, and we say \(f\) is continuous on the left at \(c\). Similarly, if \(\lim\limits_ {x\to c^+}f(x) = f(c)\), the graph connects to that point from the right, and \(f\) is continuous on the right.

To be continuous, a function must be continuous on the left and on the right: both one-sided limits must equal \(f(c)\). The graph has to be attached to the point \((c, f(c))\) from both directions.

Example 1. Find all points of discontinuity of \[f(x) = \begin{cases} 2, & x < 3 \\ 2x - 4, & 3 \le x \le 5 \\ 7 - x, & x > 5\end{cases}\]

Show solution

The pieces are all continuous, so only the transition points 3 and 5 need checking. Mark on a number line which formula applies where: 2 to the left of 3, \(2x - 4\) from 3 to 5, and \(7 - x\) beyond 5.

  1. f(3)\(3\) belongs to the middle case: \(2\cdot 3 - 4 = 2\)
  2. At 3, left\[\lim_{x\to 3^-}2 = 2\]
  3. At 3, right\[\lim_{x\to 3^+}(2x-4) = 2\cdot 3 - 4 = 2\]
  4. At 3all three equal 2, so \(f\) is continuous at \(x = 3\)
  5. f(5)\(5\) also belongs to the middle case: \(2\cdot 5 - 4 = 6\)
  6. At 5, left\[\lim_{x\to 5^-}(2x-4) = 6\]
  7. At 5, right\[\lim_{x\to 5^+}(7-x) = 2\]
  8. At 5\(6 \ne 2\), so \(f\) is not continuous at \(x = 5\)

Answerx = 5 is the only point of discontinuity

Example 2. Find all points of discontinuity of \[g(x) = \begin{cases} x/|x|, & x \ne 0 \\ 1, & x = 0\end{cases}\]

Show solution

First make sense of \(x/|x|\). The absolute value is \(x\) itself when \(x > 0\), and \(-x\) when \(x < 0\).

  1. x > 0\(\dfrac{x}{|x|} = \dfrac{x}{x} = 1\)
  2. x < 0\(\dfrac{x}{|x|} = \dfrac{x}{-x} = -1\)
  3. Elsewhereaway from 0 the function is a constant, so continuous; only \(x = 0\) needs checking
  4. g(0)given as \(1\)
  5. At 0, left\(\lim\limits_ {x\to 0^-}g(x) = -1\)
  6. At 0, right\(\lim\limits_ {x\to 0^+}g(x) = 1\)
  7. At 0\(-1 \ne 1\), so \(g\) is not continuous at \(x = 0\)

Answerx = 0 is the only point of discontinuity

\(g\) is continuous on the right at 0, since the right-hand limit equals \(g(0) = 1\), but not on the left. Choosing \(g(0) = 1\) attached the point to one side; no choice of \(g(0)\) could attach it to both, because the two sides approach different numbers.

MethodSolving inequalities with sign charts

Continuity gives a clean way to find where a function is positive or negative, that is, to solve \(f(x) > 0\) or \(f(x) < 0\). It rests on one observation.

Worth memorizing

  • A continuous function cannot change its sign without being equal to 0 somewhere.

So if we find every number where \(f\) is either discontinuous or equal to 0, then on each interval between those numbers \(f\) has constant sign. Building this picture is called the sign chart for \(f\).

  1. Find the partition numbers, where \(f\) is discontinuous or equal to 0. For a fraction: where the numerator is 0, or the denominator is 0.
  2. They divide the number line into intervals.
  3. Find the sign of \(f\) on each interval by testing it at a convenient point.

(a) Solve \(\dfrac{5x - 10}{x^2 - 1} \le 0\).    (b) Solve \(25x - x^3 > 0\).

Show solution
  1. a) Partitionnumerator \(5x - 10 = 0\) at \(x = 2\); denominator \(x^2 - 1 = 0\) at \(x = \pm 1\)
  2. a) Test\(x = -2\): \(\frac{-10-10}{4-1} < 0\);   \(x = 0\): \(\frac{-10}{-1} = 10 > 0\)
  3. a) Test\(x = 1.5\): \(\frac{7.5-10}{2.25-1} < 0\);   \(x = 3\): \(\frac{15-10}{9-1} > 0\)
  4. a) Read offnegative on \((-\infty,-1)\) and \((1,2)\); zero at \(x = 2\), which \(\le\) allows; \(\pm 1\) are excluded, since \(f\) is undefined there
  5. b) Partition\[25x - x^3 = x(25 - x^2) = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = 0,\ 5,\ -5\]
  6. b) Test\(x = -10\): \(-250 - (-1000) = 750 > 0\);   \(x = -1\): \(-25 - (-1) = -24 < 0\)
  7. b) Test\(x = 1\): \(25 - 1 = 24 > 0\);   \(x = 10\): \(250 - 1000 < 0\)
  8. b) Read offpositive on \((-\infty,-5)\) and \((0,5)\); the partition numbers themselves give 0, which \(>\) excludes

Answer(a) (−∞, −1) ∪ (1, 2]    (b) (−∞, −5) ∪ (0, 5)

Which partition numbers get a bracket

A zero of the function is included when the inequality allows equality (\(\le\) or \(\ge\)) and excluded when it is strict. A point where the function is undefined is always excluded, whatever the inequality says. That is why (a) has a bracket at 2 but parentheses at \(\pm 1\).

And pick test points you can compute in your head. Their exact values do not matter, only their signs.
