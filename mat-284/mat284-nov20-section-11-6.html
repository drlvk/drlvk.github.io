MethodOptimization word problems

Goal: learn to solve word problems involving maximization or minimization. We already have the mathematical tools we need to find the maximum or minimum of a function \(f\), but there are a few other considerations.

  1. We may have to construct the function \(f\) first: the formula stated in the problem might not be exactly the thing to maximize or minimize.
  2. The variable of \(f\) may be constrained because of what it represents. For example, if the variable is a quantity (or a price), then it must be positive, \(x > 0\).
  3. Often we do not have a finite interval \([A, B]\) for the variable, like we did in section 11.5. Then the method of plugging all the candidates for extrema into \(f\) does not work, and we need either the sign chart for \(f'\) or the second derivative test.

Fortunately, such word problems often have just one critical number, once we discard irrelevant ones per item (b). This implies that the local extremum we get at that point is also the absolute extremum. We just need to make sure it matches our objective: to maximize or to minimize.

Plan of solution

  1. Find the function to maximize (or minimize).
  2. Determine the constraints on its variable, if any. (Usually it’s positive.)
  3. Find the critical numbers.
  4. Even if there is just one critical number, we must check that it is a maximum of the function, rather than a minimum. We can use either the first derivative test (a sign chart for \(f'\)) or the second derivative test (plug the critical number into \(f''\)).

The textbook prefers the second derivative test in this section, but you can choose. At the end, check the problem statement again to make sure you answered the question(s) asked.

Either test works

Say 10 is the only critical number on \((0, \infty)\). A sign chart with \(+\) on \((0, 10)\) and \(-\) on \((10, \infty)\) shows a maximum. So does the second derivative test, if \(f''(10) < 0\): concave down.

Example 1Maximizing profit

The price-demand equation for some product is \(p = 30/\sqrt{x}\). The cost is $3 per unit. Find the price that will maximize the profit. Also find the maximal possible profit.

Show solution

Our goal is to maximize the profit function, which is not given to us: profit = revenue − cost.

  1. Revenuequantity · price \(= x \cdot \dfrac{30}{\sqrt{x}} = 30\sqrt{x}\)
  2. Cost\(3 \cdot \text{quantity} = 3x\)
  3. Profitrevenue − cost \(= 30\sqrt{x} - 3x\). This is \(f(x)\), the function to maximize.
  4. Constraintsince \(x\) is a quantity, \(x > 0\)
  5. f′\(f'(x) = 30 \cdot \frac12 x^{-1/2} - 3 = \dfrac{15}{\sqrt{x}} - 3\)
  6. Solve\(\dfrac{15}{\sqrt{x}} - 3 = 0\), so \(15 - 3\sqrt{x} = 0\), \(15 = 3\sqrt{x}\), \(\sqrt{x} = 5\), so \(x = 25\)
  7. Checkfirst derivative test: \(f'(1) = \frac{15}{1} - 3 = 12 > 0\) and \(f'(100) = \frac{15}{10} - 3 = -1.5 < 0\). Signs \((+\ -)\): a maximum.
  8. Pricere-read the question: it asks for the price. \(p = \dfrac{30}{\sqrt{25}} = \dfrac{30}{5} = 6\)
  9. Profit\(30\sqrt{25} - 3 \cdot 25 = 30 \cdot 5 - 75 = 75\)

Answerprice $6; maximal profit $75

The variable does not have to be the price

Even though the question asks for the price, it need not be our variable. We can find the optimal \(x\), and then use the demand equation to find \(p\).

Example 2Minimizing average cost

Minimizing the total cost is usually not an interesting problem: producing nothing (quantity = 0) probably achieves that. Minimizing the average cost (total/quantity) is more interesting.

The total cost is \(C(x) = x^2 + 12x + 1600\). What quantity \(x\) will minimize the average cost?

Show solution

First decide what our function \(f(x)\) should be: the average cost, not the total.

  1. Function\(f(x) = \dfrac{\text{total}}{x} = \dfrac{x^2 + 12x + 1600}{x} = x + 12 + 1600x^{-1}\)
  2. Constraintwe consider \(x > 0\), since \(x\) is a quantity
  3. f′\(f'(x) = 1 + 0 + 1600(-1)x^{-2} = 1 - \dfrac{1600}{x^2}\)
  4. Solve\(1 - \dfrac{1600}{x^2} = 0\), so \(x^2 = 1600\): \(x = 40\), or \(x = -40\), which is disregarded since we only need \(x > 0\)
  5. Checksecond derivative test: \(f''(x) = 0 - 1600(-2)x^{-3} = \dfrac{3200}{x^3}\), and \(f''(40) = \dfrac{3200}{40^3} > 0\): concave up, a minimum

Answerx = 40 minimizes the average cost

The minimal average cost itself is \(f(40) = 40 + 12 + \frac{1600}{40} = 92\).

Example 3Maximizing revenue

The price-demand equation for some product is \(x = 60e^{-0.02p}\). Find the price that will maximize the revenue.

Show solution
  1. Function\(f(p) = \text{revenue} = x \cdot p = 60p\,e^{-0.02p}\)
  2. Constraintconsider only \(p > 0\)
  3. f′product rule, with \(\left(e^{\square}\right)' = e^{\square} \cdot \square'\) and (inner)′ \(= -0.02\): \[f'(p) = (60p)'e^{-0.02p} + 60p\left(e^{-0.02p}\right)' = 60e^{-0.02p} + 60p \cdot e^{-0.02p} \cdot (-0.02) = e^{-0.02p}(60 - 1.2p)\]
  4. Solve\(e^{\square} > 0\), so \(f' = 0\) only when \(60 - 1.2p = 0\): \(p = \dfrac{60}{1.2} = 50\)
  5. Checkthe first derivative test is much better here, since \(f''\) is hard to find: \(f'(10) = e^{\square}(60 - 12) > 0\) and \(f'(100) = e^{\square}(60 - 120) < 0\). Signs \((+\ -)\): a maximum.

Answerp = $50 will maximize the revenue

The maximal revenue itself is \(60 \cdot 50 \cdot e^{-1} = 3000/e \approx \$1103.64\).

Sign of a factored derivative

Writing \(f'(p) = e^{-0.02p}(60 - 1.2p)\) makes the test easy: the exponential factor is always positive, so the sign of \(f'\) is the sign of \(60 - 1.2p\).
