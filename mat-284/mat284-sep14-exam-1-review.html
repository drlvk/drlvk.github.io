Problem ARevenue across two months

At present, in September, a manufacturer has 150 units of a product in stock. The product now sells at $15 per unit. In October the unit price will increase to $21. The manufacturer wants the total revenue to be no less than $2,600.

(a) Express this as an inequality in which \(x\) is the number of units sold in September.

(b) Solve it to find the maximum number of units that can be sold in September.

Show solution

Revenue is price times quantity, but there are two prices here, so there are two pieces of revenue. This is an allocation problem: the 150 units get split between September and October.

  1. Two variables\(x\) units sold in September, \(y\) units sold in October
  2. Two revenuesSeptember brings \(15x\), October brings \(21y\)
  3. Goal“no less than” is non-strict: \[15x + 21y \ge 2600\]
  4. Relation\[x + y = 150 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y = 150 - x\]
  5. Substitute\[15x + 21(150 - x) \ge 2600\]
  6. Expand\[15x + 3150 - 21x \ge 2600\]
  7. Collect\[-6x \ge -550\]
  8. Divide by −6the sign flips: \[x \le \frac{550}{6} = 91.666\ldots\]
  9. Whole unitsthe largest whole number at or below \(91.67\) is \(91\)

Answer15x + 21(150 − x) ≥ 2600, giving at most 91 units in September

Check: 91 in September and 59 in October give \(15(91) + 21(59) = 1365 + 1239 = 2604 \ge 2600\). ✓ And 92 units would give \(2598\), which falls short.

Why selling less in September raises revenue

October pays more per unit, so every unit held back earns an extra $6. That is why the answer is a ceiling on \(x\) rather than a floor, and why the inequality flips when you divide by \(-6\). If your answer came out as \(x \ge 91.67\), the flip went missing.

Note also that the whole-number step rounds down here. With \(x \le 91.67\), rounding up to 92 leaves the allowed range.

Problem BDomain and asymptotes

Find the domain of \(f(x) = \dfrac{\sqrt{x + 4}}{x^2 - 3x + 2}\), and find its vertical asymptotes.

Show solution

Both domain rules apply here: there is a square root and a denominator.

  1. Root\(x + 4 \ge 0\), so \(x \ge -4\)
  2. Denominator\[x^2 - 3x + 2 = 0\]
  3. Factor\(-1\) and \(-2\) multiply to \(2\) and add to \(-3\): \[(x - 1)(x - 2) = 0\]
  4. Exclude\(x = 1\) and \(x = 2\), both of which are above \(-4\), so both matter
  5. Combine\(x \ge -4\), excluding \(1\) and \(2\)

Answerdomain [−4, 1) ∪ (1, 2) ∪ (2, ∞); vertical asymptotes at x = 1 and x = 2

Domain and asymptotes are different questions

Both \(1\) and \(2\) are excluded from the domain and both are vertical asymptotes, but that is a coincidence of this problem, not a rule. The endpoint \(-4\) is a domain boundary and is not an asymptote: the function is perfectly finite there, equal to \(\frac{0}{30} = 0\).

Problem CMaking a profit

To produce one backpack, a company spends $4.25 on material and $7.75 on labor. Its fixed cost is $2,500, and each backpack sells for $18. What is the smallest number of backpacks that must be sold for the company to realize a profit?

Show solution
  1. Per unitmaterial plus labor: \(4.25 + 7.75 = 12\) per backpack
  2. Revenue\[18x\]
  3. Total cost\[2500 + 12x\]
  4. Profit\[18x - (2500 + 12x) = 18x - 2500 - 12x = 6x - 2500\]
  5. Goal“realize a profit” means strictly positive: \[6x - 2500 > 0\]
  6. Solve\[6x > 2500 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x > \frac{2500}{6} = 416.666\ldots\]
  7. Whole unitsthe smallest whole number strictly above \(416.67\) is \(417\)

Answer417 backpacks

Check: at 417 the profit is \(6(417) - 2500 = 2\) dollars, and at 416 it is \(-4\). ✓

Two costs, one rate

Material and labor are both variable costs, so add them before doing anything else. The single number 12 then behaves exactly like the variable costs in every earlier problem.

And note the rounding direction. Here \(x > 416.67\) rounds up to 417, the opposite of Problem A. The inequality sign decides, not habit.

Problem DEvaluating a piecewise function

Given \[f(x) = \begin{cases} 3x + 2, & \text{if } x < 5 \\ 8 - x, & \text{if } x \ge 5\end{cases}\] compute \(f(-2) + f(0)\) and \(\dfrac{f(6)}{f(2)}\).

Show solution

Check each input against the condition before substituting.

  1. f(−2)\(-2 < 5\), so \(3(-2) + 2 = -6 + 2 = -4\)
  2. f(0)\(0 < 5\), so \(3(0) + 2 = 2\)
  3. Sum\[-4 + 2 = -2\]
  4. f(6)\(6 \ge 5\), so the second formula: \(8 - 6 = 2\)
  5. f(2)\(2 < 5\), so the first formula: \(3(2) + 2 = 8\)
  6. Quotient\[\frac{2}{8} = \frac{1}{4}\]

Answerf(−2) + f(0) = −2, and f(6)/f(2) = 1/4

Two things \(\frac{f(6)}{f(2)}\) is not

It is not \(f(3)\). Function notation is not multiplication, so it does not cancel like a fraction of numbers. For the record \(f(3) = 11\), nothing like \(\frac{1}{4}\).

It is also not \(3\). We cannot cancel \(f\) because it stands for applying a function, not for multiplying.

Problem EWriting a piecewise function

An energy company charges a base charge of $9.00 per month, then $0.0550 per kWh for the first 1000 kWh or less, and $0.0700 per kWh for anything over 1000. Write a piecewise function \(f(x)\) for the monthly charge on \(x\) kWh.

Show solution

Split the usage at 1000 and note that the $9.00 is charged either way.

  1. Up to 1000base charge plus the low rate on all of it: \(9 + 0.055x\)
  2. Above 1000base, plus the low rate on the first 1000, plus the high rate on the excess
  3. Second case\[9 + 0.055(1000) + 0.07(x - 1000)\]
  4. Simplify\[9 + 55 + 0.07x - 70 = 0.07x - 6\]

Answerf(x) = 9 + 0.055x if x ≤ 1000, and f(x) = 0.07x − 6 if x > 1000

The same shape as the salesperson problem

Compare this with Friday's gadget problem: a flat rate on the first block, a different rate on the excess, and \(x - 1000\) in place of \(x - 100\). The only new feature is the base charge, which sits in both cases because you pay it no matter how little you use.

Problem FDegree and leading coefficient

Find the degree and leading coefficient of \(f(x) = 6x^5 + (3x^2 - x)(8 - 2x^3)\).

Show solution

It is not in expanded form, so expand first.

  1. Four products\[3x^2(8) + 3x^2(-2x^3) + (-x)(8) + (-x)(-2x^3)\]
  2. Simplify\[24x^2 - 6x^5 - 8x + 2x^4\]
  3. Add the 6x⁵\[6x^5 + 24x^2 - 6x^5 - 8x + 2x^4\]
  4. Cancel\(6x^5 - 6x^5 = 0\), so \[f(x) = 2x^4 + 24x^2 - 8x\]
  5. Read offdegree \(4\), leading coefficient \(2\)

Answerdegree 4, leading coefficient 2

This one is built as a trap

The obvious but wrong answer is degree 5 with leading coefficient 6, read straight off the \(6x^5\) sitting in front. But \(3x^2 \cdot (-2x^3) = -6x^5\), and the two fifth-power terms cancel exactly. The degree-5 term does not survive.

Problem GBreak-even quantity

Given total revenue \(R = 50x\) and total cost \(C = 35x + 3900\), find the break-even quantity.

Show solution
  1. Goalbreak even means \(\text{revenue} = \text{cost}\)
  2. Equation\[50x = 35x + 3900\]
  3. Collect\[15x = 3900\]
  4. Solve\[x = \frac{3900}{15} = 260\]

Answer260 units

Check: \(R = 50(260) = 13000\) and \(C = 35(260) + 3900 = 9100 + 3900 = 13000\). ✓

Break even is an equation, not an inequality

Compare with Problem C, where “realize a profit” gave \(> 0\) and forced a rounding decision. Here the answer comes out a whole number on its own, and nothing needs rounding.

Problem HEquilibrium, with a tax

The price–supply equation for a product is \(p - 0.06x = 50\), and the price–demand equation is \(p + 0.02x = 90\).

(a) Find the equilibrium point, both quantity and price.

(b) A tax of $10 per unit is imposed on the supplier. Find the new price–supply equation, then the new equilibrium.

Show solution

(a) Both equations solve for \(p\) in one step, so isolate \(p\) in each and set them equal.

  1. Supply\[p = 0.06x + 50\]
  2. Demand\[p = 90 - 0.02x\]
  3. Set equal\[0.06x + 50 = 90 - 0.02x\]
  4. Collect\[0.08x = 40\]
  5. Solve\[x = \frac{40}{0.08} = 500\]
  6. Back for p\[p = 0.06(500) + 50 = 30 + 50 = 80\]

Answer (a)the point (500, 80)

(b) Replace \(p\) by \((p - 10)\) in the supply equation only. The demand equation does not change.

  1. Substitute\[(p - 10) - 0.06x = 50\]
  2. New supply\[p = 0.06x + 60\]
  3. Set equal\[60 + 0.06x = 90 - 0.02x\]
  4. Collect\[0.08x = 30\]
  5. Solve\[x = \frac{30}{0.08} = 375\]
  6. Back for p\[p = 60 + 0.06(375) = 60 + 22.5 = 82.5\]

Answer (b)new supply p = 0.06x + 60; new equilibrium (375, 82.5)

The price rose $2.50, not $10

Consumers now pay $82.50 instead of $80.00, while the supplier keeps \(82.50 - 10 = 72.50\), which is $7.50 less than before. If your new price comes out exactly $10 higher, you changed the demand equation too.

Problem JFitting a supply equation

A supplier will produce 300 units when the price is $20, and 800 units when the price is $40. Find the price–supply equation, assuming it is linear. Give the answer in slope-intercept form \(p = mx + b\).

Show solution

Quantity is \(x\) and price is \(p\), so the two points are \((300,\ 20)\) and \((800,\ 40)\). Quantity comes first even though the problem states the price second.

  1. Slope\[m = \frac{40 - 20}{800 - 300} = \frac{20}{500} = 0.04\]
  2. Sign checkpositive, as a supply equation should be
  3. Point-slopethrough \((300,\ 20)\): \[p - 20 = 0.04(x - 300)\]
  4. Distribute\[p - 20 = 0.04x - 12\]
  5. Add 20\[p = 0.04x + 8\]

Answerp = 0.04x + 8

Check both points: at \(x = 300\), \(12 + 8 = 20\), and at \(x = 800\), \(32 + 8 = 40\). ✓

Read what form is being asked for

The question names slope-intercept form, so stopping at the point-slope line leaves the job half done. The same slope \(0.04 = \frac{1}{25}\) can also be written as a fraction if you prefer exact arithmetic.
