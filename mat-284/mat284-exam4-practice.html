MAT 284 Business Calculus

Exam 4 practice

Twenty problems from Sections 10.4, 10.7, 11.1 and 11.2, in mixed order. Work each one on paper first, then open the answer to check it, or the solution to see the steps.

  1. Find the derivative of \(f(x)=(7+2\ln x)^5\).

    Answer

    \(f'(x)=\dfrac{10(7+2\ln x)^4}{x}\)

    Solution

    Use the chain rule with outer function \(u^5\) and inner function \(u=7+2\ln x\), whose derivative is \(u'=\dfrac{2}{x}\):

    \[f'(x)=5(7+2\ln x)^4\cdot\frac{2}{x}=\frac{10(7+2\ln x)^4}{x}.\]

  2. The price-demand equation of a certain item is \(x=500e^{-0.04p}\), where \(p\) is the price and \(x\) is the number of items sold at that price. Evaluate the elasticity of demand \(E\) when \(p=30\), and describe the demand.

    Answer

    \(E(30)=1.2\); demand is elastic.

    Solution

    Here \(x'(p)=500e^{-0.04p}\cdot(-0.04)=-20e^{-0.04p}\), so

    \[E(p)=-\frac{p\,x'(p)}{x(p)}=-\frac{p\,(-20e^{-0.04p})}{500e^{-0.04p}}=0.04p.\]

    So \(E(30)=1.2\). Since \(E\gt 1\), demand is elastic.

  3. Find \(y'\), given \(y=5^{2x^2+x}\).

    Answer

    \(y'=5^{2x^2+x}\ln 5\cdot(4x+1)\)

    Solution

    Since \(\dfrac{d}{du}5^u=5^u\ln 5\), the chain rule with \(u=2x^2+x\) and \(u'=4x+1\) gives

    \[y'=5^{2x^2+x}\ln 5\cdot(4x+1).\]

  4. Let \(f(x)=8x^2-x^4\). Find the open intervals on which \(f\) is increasing (decreasing). Then determine the \(x\)-coordinates of all local maxima (minima).

    Answer

    Increasing on \((-\infty,-2)\) and \((0,2)\); decreasing on \((-2,0)\) and \((2,\infty)\). Local maxima at \(x=-2\) and \(x=2\); local minimum at \(x=0\).

    Solution

    \(f'(x)=16x-4x^3=-4x(x-2)(x+2)\), so the critical numbers are \(x=-2\), \(0\), \(2\). Test a point in each interval: \(f'(-3)=60\gt 0\), \(f'(-1)=-12\lt 0\), \(f'(1)=12\gt 0\), \(f'(3)=-60\lt 0\).

    So \(f\) increases on \((-\infty,-2)\) and \((0,2)\), and decreases on \((-2,0)\) and \((2,\infty)\). The derivative changes from positive to negative at \(x=\pm 2\), giving local maxima, and from negative to positive at \(x=0\), giving a local minimum.

  5. Recall that the relative rate of change of \(f\) is \(\dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\). Find the relative rate of change of each function.

    1. \(f(x)=5+2e^{-3x}\)
    2. \(f(x)=40-5x\)
    Answer

    (a) \(\dfrac{-6e^{-3x}}{5+2e^{-3x}}\), equivalently \(-\dfrac{6}{5e^{3x}+2}\)    (b) \(\dfrac{-5}{40-5x}\), equivalently \(\dfrac{1}{x-8}\)

    Solution

    (a) \(f'(x)=-6e^{-3x}\), so \(\dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}=\dfrac{-6e^{-3x}}{5+2e^{-3x}}\). Multiplying the numerator and denominator by \(e^{3x}\) gives the equivalent form \(-\dfrac{6}{5e^{3x}+2}\).

    (b) \(f'(x)=-5\), so \(\dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}=\dfrac{-5}{40-5x}\), which simplifies to \(\dfrac{1}{x-8}\).

  6. Let \(f(x)=x^{-3}e^{4x}\). Find \(f'(x)\).

    Answer

    \(f'(x)=-3x^{-4}e^{4x}+4x^{-3}e^{4x}\)

    Solution

    Use the product rule, with \(\dfrac{d}{dx}x^{-3}=-3x^{-4}\) and, by the chain rule, \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{4x}=4e^{4x}\):

    \[f'(x)=-3x^{-4}e^{4x}+x^{-3}\cdot 4e^{4x}=-3x^{-4}e^{4x}+4x^{-3}e^{4x}.\]

  7. The price-demand equation \(x=600-12p\) relates the price \(p\) and the demand \(x\). Find the elasticity of demand at each price, and describe the demand as elastic, inelastic, or unit elastic.

    1. \(p=20\)
    2. \(p=25\)
    3. \(p=30\)
    Answer

    \(E(p)=\dfrac{p}{50-p}\). (a) \(E=\tfrac23\), inelastic    (b) \(E=1\), unit elastic    (c) \(E=\tfrac32\), elastic

    Solution

    \(x'(p)=-12\), so

    \[E(p)=-\frac{p\,x'(p)}{x(p)}=\frac{12p}{600-12p}=\frac{p}{50-p}.\]

    (a) \(E(20)=\dfrac{20}{30}=\dfrac23\lt 1\): inelastic. (b) \(E(25)=\dfrac{25}{25}=1\): unit elastic. (c) \(E(30)=\dfrac{30}{20}=\dfrac32\gt 1\): elastic.

  8. Let \(f(x)=5e^{-2x^2}\). Identify the locations of the local extrema and of the inflection points of \(f\). Write “none” if any of these do not exist.

    1. Local minimum
    2. Local maximum
    3. Inflection points
    Answer

    (a) none    (b) \(x=0\)    (c) \(x=-\tfrac12\) and \(x=\tfrac12\)

    Solution

    By the chain rule, \(f'(x)=5e^{-2x^2}\cdot(-4x)=-20xe^{-2x^2}\). Since \(e^{-2x^2}\gt 0\), \(f'(x)=0\) only at \(x=0\). Also \(f'(x)\gt 0\) for \(x\lt 0\) and \(f'(x)\lt 0\) for \(x\gt 0\), so there is a local maximum at \(x=0\) and no local minimum.

    By the product rule,

    \[f''(x)=-20e^{-2x^2}+(-20x)(-4x)e^{-2x^2}=20e^{-2x^2}(4x^2-1).\]

    This is zero at \(x=\pm\tfrac12\), and \(4x^2-1\) changes sign at each of these points, so both are inflection points.

  9. Find \(y'\), given \(y=\log_3(4x^2-1)\).

    Answer

    \(y'=\dfrac{8x}{(4x^2-1)\ln 3}\)

    Solution

    Since \(\dfrac{d}{du}\log_3 u=\dfrac{1}{u\ln 3}\), the chain rule with \(u=4x^2-1\) and \(u'=8x\) gives

    \[y'=\frac{1}{(4x^2-1)\ln 3}\cdot 8x=\frac{8x}{(4x^2-1)\ln 3}.\]

  10. Use the price-demand equation \(p+0.002x=60\), where \(0\le p\le 60\), for both parts.

    1. Find the elasticity of demand when \(p=\$15\). If the $15 price is increased by 6%, what is the approximate percentage change in demand?
    2. Find the elasticity of demand when \(p=\$45\). If the $45 price is decreased by 4%, what is the approximate percentage change in demand?
    Answer

    \(E(p)=\dfrac{p}{60-p}\). (a) \(E=\tfrac13\); demand changes by about \(-2\%\)    (b) \(E=3\); demand changes by about \(+12\%\)

    Solution

    Solve for \(x\): \(x=\dfrac{60-p}{0.002}=500(60-p)\), so \(x'(p)=-500\) and

    \[E(p)=-\frac{p\,x'(p)}{x(p)}=\frac{500p}{500(60-p)}=\frac{p}{60-p}.\]

    The percentage change in demand is approximately \(-E\) times the percentage change in price.

    (a) \(E(15)=\dfrac{15}{45}=\dfrac13\), so a 6% price increase changes demand by about \(-\tfrac13\cdot 6\%=-2\%\).

    (b) \(E(45)=\dfrac{45}{15}=3\), so a 4% price decrease changes demand by about \(-3\cdot(-4\%)=+12\%\).

  11. Let \(f(x)=\ln\left[\dfrac{x^6(x+5)^4}{(x^2+1)^3}\right]\). Differentiate \(f\). (Suggestion: rewrite before differentiating.)

    Answer

    \(f'(x)=\dfrac6x+\dfrac{4}{x+5}-\dfrac{6x}{x^2+1}\)

    Solution

    By the properties of logarithms,

    \[f(x)=6\ln x+4\ln(x+5)-3\ln(x^2+1).\]

    Differentiate term by term, using the chain rule on the last term:

    \[f'(x)=\frac6x+\frac{4}{x+5}-3\cdot\frac{2x}{x^2+1}=\frac6x+\frac{4}{x+5}-\frac{6x}{x^2+1}.\]

  12. Let \(f(x)=-x^4+2x^3+5x-1\). Find the open intervals on which \(f\) is concave up (down). Then determine the \(x\)-coordinates of all inflection points of \(f\).

    Answer

    Concave up on \((0,1)\); concave down on \((-\infty,0)\) and \((1,\infty)\). Inflection points at \(x=0\) and \(x=1\).

    Solution

    \(f'(x)=-4x^3+6x^2+5\) and \(f''(x)=-12x^2+12x=-12x(x-1)\), which is zero at \(x=0\) and \(x=1\). Test points: \(f''(-1)=-24\lt 0\), \(f''(\tfrac12)=3\gt 0\), \(f''(2)=-24\lt 0\).

    So \(f\) is concave down on \((-\infty,0)\), concave up on \((0,1)\), and concave down on \((1,\infty)\). Concavity changes at \(x=0\) and at \(x=1\), so both are inflection points.

  13. Find \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\left[\dfrac{\ln(5+x^2)}{3x}\right]\). (Suggestion: rewrite this quotient as a product.)

    Answer

    \(\dfrac{2}{3(5+x^2)}-\dfrac{\ln(5+x^2)}{3x^2}\)

    Solution

    Write the function as \(\tfrac13\,x^{-1}\ln(5+x^2)\). By the chain rule, \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\ln(5+x^2)=\dfrac{2x}{5+x^2}\). The product rule then gives

    \[\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac13\,x^{-1}\ln(5+x^2)\right] =\frac13\left[-x^{-2}\ln(5+x^2)+x^{-1}\cdot\frac{2x}{5+x^2}\right] =\frac{2}{3(5+x^2)}-\frac{\ln(5+x^2)}{3x^2}.\]

  14. Let \(f(x)=12\sqrt{x}-3x\) for \(x\gt 0\). Find the open intervals on which \(f\) is increasing (decreasing). Then determine the \(x\)-coordinates of all local maxima (minima).

    Answer

    Increasing on \((0,4)\); decreasing on \((4,\infty)\). Local maximum at \(x=4\); no local minimum.

    Solution

    \(f'(x)=12\cdot\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}-3=\dfrac{6}{\sqrt{x}}-3\). Setting \(f'(x)=0\) gives \(\sqrt{x}=2\), so \(x=4\).

    For \(0\lt x\lt 4\), \(\sqrt{x}\lt 2\), so \(f'(x)\gt 0\); for \(x\gt 4\), \(f'(x)\lt 0\). So \(f\) increases on \((0,4)\) and decreases on \((4,\infty)\), with a local maximum at \(x=4\) and no local minimum.

  15. Given the price-demand equation \(p=\dfrac{3200}{x^2}\), find the elasticity of demand when \(x=40\), and describe the demand. (Hint: first, solve the equation for \(x\).)

    Answer

    \(E=\tfrac12\); demand is inelastic.

    Solution

    Solve for \(x\), with \(x\gt 0\): \(x^2=\dfrac{3200}{p}\), so \(x=\sqrt{3200}\,p^{-1/2}\). Then \(x'(p)=-\tfrac12\sqrt{3200}\,p^{-3/2}\) and

    \[E(p)=-\frac{p\,x'(p)}{x(p)}=\frac{\tfrac12\sqrt{3200}\,p^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{3200}\,p^{-1/2}}=\frac12.\]

    When \(x=40\), \(p=\dfrac{3200}{1600}=2\), and \(E=\tfrac12\lt 1\): demand is inelastic. In fact \(E=\tfrac12\) at every price.

  16. Let \(f(x)=\dfrac{3x+2}{x-1}\). Find the open intervals on which \(f\) is concave up (down). Then determine the \(x\)-coordinates of all inflection points of \(f\).

    Answer

    Concave up on \((1,\infty)\); concave down on \((-\infty,1)\). No inflection points.

    Solution

    By the quotient rule, \(f'(x)=\dfrac{3(x-1)-(3x+2)}{(x-1)^2}=\dfrac{-5}{(x-1)^2}=-5(x-1)^{-2}\), so \(f''(x)=10(x-1)^{-3}=\dfrac{10}{(x-1)^3}\).

    This is positive for \(x\gt 1\) and negative for \(x\lt 1\): \(f\) is concave up on \((1,\infty)\) and concave down on \((-\infty,1)\). Concavity changes at \(x=1\), but \(f\) is not defined there, so there are no inflection points.

  17. Let \(f(x)=4+12x-x^3\). Find the open intervals on which \(f\) is increasing (decreasing). Then determine the \(x\)-coordinates of all local maxima (minima).

    Answer

    Increasing on \((-2,2)\); decreasing on \((-\infty,-2)\) and \((2,\infty)\). Local maximum at \(x=2\); local minimum at \(x=-2\).

    Solution

    \(f'(x)=12-3x^2=-3(x-2)(x+2)\), which is zero at \(x=\pm 2\). Test points: \(f'(-3)=-15\lt 0\), \(f'(0)=12\gt 0\), \(f'(3)=-15\lt 0\).

    So \(f\) decreases on \((-\infty,-2)\), increases on \((-2,2)\), and decreases on \((2,\infty)\): a local minimum at \(x=-2\) and a local maximum at \(x=2\).

  18. Revenue. A dairy finds that the demand for a new line of ice cream is given approximately by \(p=12e^{-x/4}\), \(x\gt 0\), where \(x\) thousand quarts are sold per week at a price of \(p\) dollars each.

    1. Find the revenue \(R(x)\), in thousands of dollars.
    2. On which interval is the graph of the revenue function concave upward? On which interval is it concave downward?
    Answer

    (a) \(R(x)=12xe^{-x/4}\)    (b) Concave upward on \((8,\infty)\); concave downward on \((0,8)\)

    Solution

    (a) \(R(x)=xp=12xe^{-x/4}\).

    (b) By the product and chain rules, \(R'(x)=12e^{-x/4}-3xe^{-x/4}=e^{-x/4}(12-3x)\). Differentiating again,

    \[R''(x)=-\tfrac14e^{-x/4}(12-3x)+e^{-x/4}(-3)=e^{-x/4}\left(\tfrac34x-6\right)=\tfrac34e^{-x/4}(x-8).\]

    Since \(e^{-x/4}\gt 0\), \(R''(x)\lt 0\) on \((0,8)\) and \(R''(x)\gt 0\) on \((8,\infty)\).

  19. Find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) for \(y=\dfrac{1}{(4x^3+5)^5}\).

    Answer

    \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac{60x^2}{(4x^3+5)^6}\)

    Solution

    Write \(y=(4x^3+5)^{-5}\). By the chain rule,

    \[\frac{dy}{dx}=-5(4x^3+5)^{-6}\cdot 12x^2=-\frac{60x^2}{(4x^3+5)^6}.\]

  20. Let \(f(x)=-\left(2x+\dfrac{18}{x}\right)\). Find the open intervals on which \(f\) is increasing (decreasing). Then determine the \(x\)-coordinates of all local maxima (minima).

    Answer

    Increasing on \((-3,0)\) and \((0,3)\); decreasing on \((-\infty,-3)\) and \((3,\infty)\). Local maximum at \(x=3\); local minimum at \(x=-3\).

    Solution

    \(f(x)=-2x-18x^{-1}\), so \(f'(x)=-2+18x^{-2}=\dfrac{18-2x^2}{x^2}=-\dfrac{2(x-3)(x+3)}{x^2}\). The critical numbers are \(x=\pm 3\). Since \(f\) is not defined at \(x=0\), the intervals are split there too.

    Test points: \(f'(-4)=-\tfrac78\lt 0\), \(f'(-1)=16\gt 0\), \(f'(1)=16\gt 0\), \(f'(4)=-\tfrac78\lt 0\). So \(f\) decreases on \((-\infty,-3)\) and \((3,\infty)\), and increases on \((-3,0)\) and \((0,3)\): a local minimum at \(x=-3\) and a local maximum at \(x=3\).
