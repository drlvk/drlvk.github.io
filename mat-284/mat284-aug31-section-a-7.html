DefinitionStandard form

Every quadratic equation can be brought into standard form

\[ax^2 + bx + c = 0\]

with everything on one side and zero on the other. Getting there first is what lets you read off \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) for any of the methods below.

Put \(3x = 7 - 5x^2\) into standard form and identify \(a\), \(b\), \(c\).

Show solution
  1. Move termsadd \(5x^2\) and subtract \(7\) on both sides
  2. Standard form\[5x^2 + 3x - 7 = 0\]
  3. Read off\(a = 5\), \(b = 3\), \(c = -7\)

Answer5x² + 3x − 7 = 0, with a = 5, b = 3, c = −7

Watch the sign of c

\(c\) is \(-7\), not \(7\). The sign belongs to the coefficient, and dropping it here is what produces a wrong discriminant three steps later.

MethodNo first-power term: take a square root

If there is no term with \(x\) to the first power, isolate \(x^2\) and take a square root. Remember that this gives two answers.

Solve \(5x^2 - 7 = 0\).

Show solution
  1. Add 7\[5x^2 = 7\]
  2. Divide by 5\[x^2 = \frac{7}{5}\]
  3. Square root\[x = \sqrt{\frac{7}{5}} \quad\text{or}\quad x = -\sqrt{\frac{7}{5}}\]

Answerx = ±√(7/5) ≈ ±1.183

Both signs

\(x^2 = \frac{7}{5}\) has two solutions, because squaring destroys the sign. Writing only the positive root loses half the answer, unless the context of a word problem rules the negative one out.

MethodNo constant term: factor

Factoring rests on one fact: a product of two numbers is 0 exactly when one of them is 0. So once the equation reads (something)(something) \(= 0\), you set each factor to zero separately.

When there is no constant term, every term carries an \(x\), so \(x\) is a common factor and the factoring is immediate.

Solve \(5x^2 + 3x = 0\).

Show solution
  1. Common factorboth terms have an \(x\): \[x(5x + 3) = 0\]
  2. Splitso either \(x = 0\) or \(5x + 3 = 0\)
  3. Solve each\[x = 0 \quad\text{or}\quad x = -\frac{3}{5}\]

Answerx = 0 or x = −3/5

Don't divide by x

Dividing both sides by \(x\) turns \(5x^2 + 3x = 0\) into \(5x + 3 = 0\) and quietly throws away the solution \(x = 0\). Factor instead of dividing whenever the thing you would divide by could itself be zero.

Poll question from class

Factoring also helps with quadratics that do have a constant term.

Factor \(x^2 + x - 6\) as \((x \;\ldots)(x \;\ldots)\), then solve \(x^2 + x - 6 = 0\).

Show solution

Look for two numbers whose product is \(-6\) and whose sum is \(1\). Those are \(3\) and \(-2\).

  1. Factor\[x^2 + x - 6 = (x + 3)(x - 2)\]
  2. Set to zero\[(x + 3)(x - 2) = 0\]
  3. Spliteither \(x + 3 = 0\) or \(x - 2 = 0\)
  4. Solve each\[x = -3 \quad\text{or}\quad x = 2\]

Answer(x + 3)(x − 2), so x = −3 or x = 2

ToolThe quadratic formula

Trying to factor \(5x^2 + 3x - 7 = 0\) would not be productive. There is no pair of tidy integers to find. For equations like this one, use the formula.

Worth memorizing

  • The equation \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\) has solutions \[x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\]

Solve \(5x^2 + 3x - 7 = 0\).

Show solution
  1. Read off\(a = 5\), \(b = 3\), \(c = -7\)
  2. Substitute\[x = \frac{-3 \pm \sqrt{3^2 - 4 \cdot 5 \cdot (-7)}}{2 \cdot 5}\]
  3. Discriminant\[3^2 - 4 \cdot 5 \cdot (-7) = 9 + 140 = 149\]
  4. Simplify\[x = \frac{-3 \pm \sqrt{149}}{10}\]
  5. Decimals\(\sqrt{149} \approx 12.2066\), so \(x \approx 0.921\) or \(x \approx -1.521\)

Answerx = (−3 ± √149)/10 ≈ 0.921 or −1.521

Two minus signs make a plus

With \(c = -7\), the term \(-4ac\) becomes \(-4 \cdot 5 \cdot (-7) = +140\).

MethodQuadratics in word problems

Word problems may lead to quadratic equations. When they do, one extra step appears at the end: check whether both solutions make sense. If \(x\) is a quantity that cannot logically be negative, a negative solution is discarded.

Example 1: lowering the price to raise sales

To sell more units it may be necessary to charge less, so the price itself depends on the quantity. Multiplying price by quantity then produces an \(x^2\).

Consumers will purchase \(x\) units of a product if the unit price is \(\dfrac{70 - x}{2}\). How many units must be sold for the revenue to be $500?

Show solution
  1. Goal\[\text{revenue} = 500\]
  2. Revenueprice times quantity: \[\left(\frac{70 - x}{2}\right)\cdot x = \frac{70x - x^2}{2}\]
  3. Equation\[\frac{70x - x^2}{2} = 500\]
  4. Times 2\[70x - x^2 = 1000\]
  5. Standard form\[x^2 - 70x + 1000 = 0\]
  6. Factor\(-50\) and \(-20\) multiply to \(1000\) and add to \(-70\): \[(x - 50)(x - 20) = 0\]
  7. Solve\[x = 50 \quad\text{or}\quad x = 20\]

Answer20 units or 50 units

Both make sense here, so both are answers. At \(x = 20\) the price is \(\frac{70-20}{2} = 25\) and the revenue is \(25 \cdot 20 = 500\). At \(x = 50\) the price is \(10\) and the revenue is \(10 \cdot 50 = 500\).

Same revenue, two ways

Selling 20 units at $25 and selling 50 units at $10 bring in the same $500.

Example 2: economies of scale

Producing more can lower the cost per unit, which is represented by an \(x^2\) part of the cost formula below.

A manufacturer sells each unit for 50 dollars. The total cost of producing \(x\) units is \(600 + 25x - 0.5x^2\) dollars. At what production level does the company break even?

Show solution
  1. Goalbreak even means \(\text{revenue} = \text{cost}\)
  2. Equation\[50x = 600 + 25x - 0.5x^2\]
  3. One side\[0.5x^2 + 25x - 600 = 0\]
  4. Times 2clears the decimal: \[x^2 + 50x - 1200 = 0\]
  5. Read off\(a = 1\), \(b = 50\), \(c = -1200\)
  6. Discriminant\[50^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot (-1200) = 2500 + 4800 = 7300\]
  7. Formula\[x = \frac{-50 \pm \sqrt{7300}}{2}\]
  8. Reject\(\sqrt{7300} \approx 85.44\), so the two roots are \(\approx 17.72\) and \(\approx -67.72\). A negative production level is impossible.

Answerx = (−50 + √7300)/2 ≈ 17.72 units

Check: at \(x = 17.72\) the revenue is \(50(17.72) = 886.0\) and the cost is \(600 + 25(17.72) - 0.5(17.72)^2 = 886.0\). ✓

Multiply through to clear decimals

Going from \(0.5x^2 + 25x - 600 = 0\) to \(x^2 + 50x - 1200 = 0\) costs one line and makes \(a = 1\), which keeps the discriminant arithmetic manageable. Multiplying both sides by the same non-zero number never changes the solutions.

Worked exampleA geometry problem

Not all word problems involve money. Areas are a natural source of quadratics, because an area is a product of two lengths that both grow.

A college parking lot is 120 ft long and 80 ft wide. The college wants to increase the area of the lot by 56% by adding strips of equal width to one end and one side, so the new shape is a larger rectangle. Find the width of one such strip.

Show solution

Let \(x\) be the width of a strip, in feet. One strip goes on the end and one on the side, so the new rectangle is \((120 + x)\) by \((80 + x)\).

  1. Current area\[120 \cdot 80 = 9600\]
  2. Up by 56%\[\left(1 + \frac{56}{100}\right)\cdot 9600 = 14976\]
  3. Goal\[\text{new area} = 14976\]
  4. Equation\[(120 + x)(80 + x) = 14976\]
  5. Expand\[9600 + 120x + 80x + x^2 = 14976\]
  6. Standard form\[x^2 + 200x - 5376 = 0\]
  7. Discriminant\[200^2 + 4 \cdot 5376 = 40000 + 21504 = 61504 = 248^2\]
  8. Formula\[x = \frac{-200 \pm 248}{2}\]
  9. Rejectthe roots are \(24\) and \(-224\); a width cannot be negative

Answer24 ft

Check: \((120 + 24)(80 + 24) = 144 \cdot 104 = 14976\), which is 56% more than 9600. ✓

One strip on each of two sides

Both dimensions grow by the same \(x\), so the two binomials share the same unknown and the product gives an \(x^2\). Draw the rectangle with the strips marked before writing anything: it is the fastest way to see that the new sides are \(120 + x\) and \(80 + x\), not \(120 + 2x\) and \(80 + 2x\).

Worked exampleCompound interest

When an interest rate appears in a formula, the percent sign is not used. A rate of 4% enters the formula as \(0.04\).

Let \(P\) be the principal and \(r\) the interest rate compounded annually. After one year the account has earned \(rP\) in interest, so the balance is

\[P + rP = (1 + r)P\]

After the second year we multiply by \(1 + r\) again, so the balance grows to \((1 + r)^2 P\), and so on. For a 4% rate the multiplier is \(1.04\) each year.

At what interest rate will $500 grow to $570 in two years?

Show solution

Here \(P = 500\), the number of years is 2, and the new balance is 570.

  1. Equation\[500(1 + r)^2 = 570\]
  2. Divide by 500\[(1 + r)^2 = \frac{570}{500} = 1.14\]
  3. Square root\(1 + r > 0\), so only the positive root applies: \[1 + r = \sqrt{1.14} \approx 1.06771\]
  4. Solve\[r \approx 0.06771\]
  5. As a percentmultiply by 100: about \(6.77\%\)

Answerabout 6.77% per year

Check: \(500(1.06771)^2 = 570.0\). ✓

Show the same thing by expanding

Expanding first also works, and it is worth seeing once so the standard form is familiar.

  1. Expand\[500(1 + 2r + r^2) = 570\]
  2. Divide by 500\[1 + 2r + r^2 = \frac{570}{500}\]
  3. Standard form\[r^2 + 2r + \left(1 - \frac{570}{500}\right) = 0, \quad\text{that is}\quad r^2 + 2r - \frac{70}{500} = 0\]
  4. Discriminant\(1 - \frac{570}{500} = -0.14\), so \[2^2 - 4(1)(-0.14) = 4 + 0.56 = 4.56\]
  5. Formula\[r = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{4.56}}{2}\]
  6. Rejectthe roots are \(\approx 0.0677\) and \(\approx -2.0677\); a rate of \(-207\%\) is meaningless here

Answerr ≈ 0.0677, the same 6.77%

Square root beats the formula here

Because the equation is \((1 + r)^2 = \text{number}\) with no loose first-power term, the square-root method from earlier in the lecture applies directly and skips the whole discriminant. Expanding is not wrong, only longer, and it introduces a subtraction of fractions where a digit is easy to lose.
