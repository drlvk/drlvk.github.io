DefinitionWhat makes an equation linear

An equation or an inequality is called linear if the variable (the unknown) is not raised to a power, does not appear in a denominator, and does not sit inside some function.

The shape to look for

  • (coefficient) \(\cdot\) (variable) \(\pm\) (number) \(=\) (number)
  • Disqualifying: \(x^2\), \(\dfrac{7}{x}\), \(\sqrt{u}\), and anything else wrapping the variable

Which of the following equations are linear?

A) \(6x^2 + 3x + 2 = 0\)    B) \(3t + 2 = 7/4\)    C) \(3x + 2 = 7/x\)

D) \(3y + 9 = 7(4 - y)\)    E) \(3\sqrt{u + 9} = 7u\)    F) \(\dfrac{x}{3} + \sqrt{9} = \dfrac{4 - x}{7}\)

Show solution
  1. ANot linear. The \(x^2\) term.
  2. BLinear. Coefficient times variable, plus a number, equals a number. \(7/4\) is just a number.
  3. CNot linear. \(\frac{7}{x}\) puts \(x\) in the denominator.
  4. DLinear. Distribute and it becomes \(3y + 9 = 28 - 7y\).
  5. ENot linear. \(u\) sits under a square root.
  6. FLinear. No \(x\) in a denominator — the 3 and the 7 are constants — and \(\sqrt{9}\) is just \(3\).

AnswerB, D, F

Watch out

A square root or a fraction does not by itself break linearity. Ask only one question: is the variable the thing being squared, divided into, or fed to a function? In F, \(\sqrt{9}\) contains no variable and \(\frac{x}{3}\) has the variable on top, so F is linear. In E, the \(u\) is under the root, so E is not.

MethodSolving linear equations

Isolate the variable on one side. To get there you may add, subtract, multiply, or divide by the same quantity on both sides.

Equation B

Solve \(3t + 2 = 7/4\).

Show solution
  1. Subtract 2\[3t = \frac{7}{4} - 2\]
  2. Combine\[3t = -\frac{1}{4} \quad\text{or}\quad 3t = -0.25\]
  3. Divide by 3\[t = \frac{-1/4}{3} = -\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{3} = -\frac{1}{12}\]

Answert = −1/12

Dividing a fraction by a number

\(\dfrac{-1/4}{3}\) is not \(-\dfrac{3}{4}\). Dividing by 3 means multiplying by \(\frac{1}{3}\), so the 3 lands on the denominator: \(-\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{3} = -\frac{1}{12}\).

Equation D

Solve \(3y + 9 = 7(4 - y)\).

Show solution
  1. Distribute\[3y + 9 = 28 - 7y\]
  2. Add 7y\[3y + 7y + 9 = 28\]
  3. Combine\[3y + 7y = (3 + 7)y = 10y \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 10y + 9 = 28\]
  4. Subtract 9\[10y = 19\]
  5. Divide by 10\[y = 1.9\]

Answery = 1.9

Distribute to both terms

\(7(4 - y)\) is \(7\cdot 4 - 7\cdot y\). The most common slip is multiplying only the first term and writing \(28 - y\).

NotationInequalities and intervals

We are not solving inequalities today, just fixing the notation.

Strict and non-strict

  • \(a < b\) means that \(a\) is less than \(b\); on the number line \(a\) is to the left of \(b\)
  • \(a > b\) means that \(a\) is greater than \(b\); on the number line \(a\) is to the right of \(b\)
  • \(a \le b\) means that either \(a < b\) or \(a = b\)
  • \(a \ge b\) means that either \(a > b\) or \(a = b\)

The first two are strict; the last two are non-strict. The difference is only about the endpoint: \(x \ge 7\) allows \(7\), while \(x > 7\) does not.

For intervals, a square bracket means included and a round parenthesis means not included. Infinity always gets a parenthesis — you can never reach it, so it can never be included.

Inequality to interval
InequalityInterval
\(a < x < b\)\((a, b)\)
\(a \le x \le b\)\([a, b]\)
\(a \le x < b\)\([a, b)\)
\(x > a\)\((a, \infty)\)
\(x \ge a\)\([a, \infty)\)
\(x \le b\)\((-\infty, b]\)
\(x < b\)\((-\infty, b)\)

The interval \((-\infty, \infty)\) is the entire number line.

Writing statements as intervals

Express each of the following in interval notation.

a) \(x > 5\)    b) \(x > 5\) and \(x \le 10\)

Show solution
  1. a)Everything to the right of 5, with 5 itself excluded: \((5, \infty)\)
  2. b)Right of 5, up to and including 10: \((5, 10]\)

Answer(5, ∞) and (5, 10]

Draw the number line

Sketching the line and shading the region takes five seconds and catches almost every bracket-direction mistake. Mark an excluded endpoint with an open circle and an included one with a filled circle.

Unions

An and statement describes one stretch of the line. An or statement can describe two stretches with a gap between them, and those are joined with the union symbol \(\cup\), as in \((1, 3) \cup [7, 11]\).

Express each of the following in interval notation.

a) \(x \le 5\) or \(x > 10\)    b) \(x \ne 4\)

Show solution
  1. a) Left piece\(x \le 5\) is \((-\infty, 5]\) — 5 included
  2. a) Right piece\(x > 10\) is \((10, \infty)\) — 10 excluded
  3. a) Join\[(-\infty, 5] \cup (10, \infty)\]
  4. b) Rewrite\(x \ne 4\) says \(x < 4\) or \(x > 4\) — the whole line with one point removed
  5. b) Join\[(-\infty, 4) \cup (4, \infty)\]

Answer(−∞, 5] ∪ (10, ∞)  and  (−∞, 4) ∪ (4, ∞)

And vs. or

And means both conditions hold at once, so you keep the overlap — usually one interval. Or means at least one holds, so you keep everything either one covers — often two pieces joined by \(\cup\).

Worked exampleTax brackets

Word problems hinge on which words are strict and which are not. Greater than is strict; does not exceed is not.

According to the IRS, you are in the 22% tax bracket if your 2025 income is greater than $48,475 but does not exceed $103,350. Express this condition two ways: as an inequality, and as an interval.

Show solution
  1. Let\(x =\) your 2025 income, in dollars
  2. Greater thanstrict, so \(48475 < x\)
  3. Not exceednon-strict, so \(x \le 103350\)
  4. Inequality\[48475 < x \le 103350\]
  5. Interval\[(48475,\ 103350]\]

Answer48,475 < x ≤ 103,350  is  (48475, 103350]

The bracket shapes mirror the inequality signs exactly: the strict \(<\) becomes a parenthesis, the non-strict \(\le\) becomes a square bracket.

The top bracket (37%) is for income exceeding $626,350. Express this as an inequality and as an interval.

Show solution
  1. Exceedingstrict, and there is no upper limit
  2. Inequality\[x > 626350\]
  3. Interval\[(626350,\ \infty)\]

Answerx > 626,350  is  (626350, ∞)

Words that set the bracket

Strict, parenthesis: greater than, less than, exceeds, more than, under.

Non-strict, square bracket: at least, at most, no more than, does not exceed, up to and including.
