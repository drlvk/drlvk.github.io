Properties of logarithms

Warm-upLogarithms grow very slowly

Logarithmic functions grow very slowly, because they are the inverse of exponential functions, which grow very fast. Undoing something explosive gives you something sluggish.

An internet company finds that when their website has \(n\) monthly visitors, the cost of keeping it up is

\[f(n) = 40\log\!\left(10 + \frac{n}{5000}\right) \text{ dollars per month.}\]

(a) If they spend $80 per month, how many visitors do they get?

(b) How much will they pay with a million monthly visitors?

Show solution

Part (a) needs solving for \(n\), so isolate the logarithm and switch to exponential form. Part (b) is just substitution.

  1. a) Set up\[80 = 40\log\!\left(10 + \frac{n}{5000}\right)\]
  2. a) Divide by 40\[2 = \log\!\left(10 + \frac{n}{5000}\right)\]
  3. a) Switcha bare \(\log\) has base 10: \[10^2 = 10 + \frac{n}{5000}\]
  4. a) Simplify\[100 = 10 + \frac{n}{5000} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 90 = \frac{n}{5000}\]
  5. a) Solve\[n = 90 \cdot 5000 = 450{,}000\]
  6. b) Substitute\[40\log\!\left(10 + \frac{1{,}000{,}000}{5000}\right) = 40\log(10 + 200)\]
  7. b) Compute\[= 40\log(210) \approx 92.89\]

Answer450,000 visitors; about $92.89 per month

What those two numbers mean together

Going from 450,000 visitors to 1,000,000 more than doubles the traffic and adds under $13 to the bill. That is what “grows very slowly” looks like in practice, and it is why logarithms show up in cost models that are supposed to scale well.

Notice the two directions of work: (a) had the unknown inside the logarithm, so it needed the exponential form; (b) had the unknown supplied, so it needed nothing but substitution.

OrientationWhy logarithms exist

Some arithmetic operations are easier than others.

  1. Addition is the easiest.
  2. Multiplication is harder; it is repeated addition.
  3. Raising to a power is harder still; it is repeated multiplication.

Compare the three for the same pair of numbers:

The same two numbers, three operations
OperationResult
\(12 + 34\)46
\(12 \cdot 34\)408
\(12^{34}\)4922235242952026704037113243122008064

Logarithms were invented to reduce the arithmetic complexity: with them you can replace multiplication by addition, and exponentiation by multiplication.

This does not mean 12 · 34 becomes 12 + 34

Logarithms convert the multiplication of two numbers into the addition of two different numbers. The product 408 does not turn into 46. What happens is that \(\log 408\) turns into \(\log 12 + \log 34\), and those two logarithms are not 12 and 34.

MethodExponentials: simpler on the inside

Recall the exponent laws from section 2.5, and read each one from left to right, watching which side gets the harder operation.

Multiplication outside becomes addition inside

  • \(b^x b^y = b^{x+y}\)   — a product between, an addition in the exponent
  • \(\dfrac{b^x}{b^y} = b^{x-y}\)   — a division between, a subtraction in the exponent
  • \(\left(b^x\right)^y = b^{xy}\)   — a power outside, a multiplication in the exponent

For example \(\left(12^{34}\right)^{10} = 12^{340}\): raising to the tenth power outside costs nothing more than multiplying \(34\) by \(10\) inside.

So for exponential functions, things are simpler on the inside. Each operation drops one level of difficulty when it moves into the exponent.

ToolLogarithms: simpler on the outside

A logarithm is the inverse of an exponential, so it turns all of those properties inside out. Every rule above runs backwards.

Worth memorizing

  • \(\log_b(mn) = \log_b m + \log_b n\)   — the logarithm of a product is the sum of logarithms
  • \(\log_b\!\left(\dfrac{m}{n}\right) = \log_b m - \log_b n\)   — the logarithm of a quotient is the difference
  • \(\log_b(m^r) = r\log_b m\)   — an exponent inside comes out as a factor

These hold for every base. Some numerical checks:

The three laws, verified
LawCheck
product\(\log(12 \cdot 34) = \log 408 = 2.61066\), and \(\log 12 + \log 34 = 2.61066\)
quotient\(\log_2 48 - \log_2 12 = \log_2\frac{48}{12} = \log_2 4 = 2\)
power\(\log(12^{34}) = 34\log 12 \approx 36.69\)

The power law as a size estimate

From \(\log(12^{34}) \approx 36.69\) you can read off how big \(12^{34}\) is without computing it: since \(36 < 36.69 < 37\), we get \(10^{36} < 12^{34} < 10^{37}\). So it is a 37-digit number.

The duality in one place

  • Exponentials are simpler on the inside: the hard operation sits outside, the easy one in the exponent.
  • Logarithms are simpler on the outside: the hard operation sits inside, the easy one outside.

MethodExpanding logarithms

To expand a logarithm means to write it as a combination of simpler logarithms, using the three laws.

Express \(\log 30\) in another way, using the arithmetic of the number 30.

Show solution

Factor 30 and apply the product law. There is more than one right answer.

  1. Factor\[\log 30 = \log(3 \cdot 10) = \log 3 + \log 10\]
  2. Simplify\(\log 10 = 1\), since \(\log_b b = 1\): \[= \log 3 + 1\]
  3. Or further\(10 = 2 \cdot 5\), so \[\log 30 = \log 3 + \log 2 + \log 5\]
  4. Or differently\(30 = 5 \cdot 6\), so \[\log 30 = \log 5 + \log 6\]

Answerlog 3 + 1, or log 2 + log 3 + log 5, among others

The \(\log 10 = 1\) step is the useful one: whenever the base appears inside its own logarithm, it collapses to 1.

What can we do with \(\ln 1{,}000{,}000{,}000{,}000\), the natural log of one trillion?

Show solution
  1. Rewriteone trillion is \(10^{12}\)
  2. Power law\[\ln\!\left(10^{12}\right) = 12\ln 10 \approx 27.631\]

Answer12 ln 10 ≈ 27.631

Note that \(\ln 10\) does not simplify to 1 here. That collapse only happens when the number inside matches the base, and the base of \(\ln\) is \(e\), not 10.

Expanding formulas

Expand a) \(\log(5x^3)\)    b) \(\ln\dfrac{x+5}{x^3}\)    c) \(\ln\dfrac{(x+1)^3}{x\sqrt{x+1}}\)

Show solution
  1. a) Product\(5x^3\) is \(5\) times \(x^3\): \[\log(5x^3) = \log 5 + \log(x^3)\]
  2. a) Power\[= \log 5 + 3\log x\]
  3. b) Quotient\[\ln\frac{x+5}{x^3} = \ln(x+5) - \ln(x^3)\]
  4. b) Power\[= \ln(x+5) - 3\ln x\]
  5. c) Quotient\[\ln\frac{(x+1)^3}{x\sqrt{x+1}} = \ln\!\left((x+1)^3\right) - \ln\!\left(x\sqrt{x+1}\right)\]
  6. c) Productthe bottom is a product, so it splits: \[= 3\ln(x+1) - \Big(\ln x + \ln\sqrt{x+1}\Big)\]
  7. c) Root\(\sqrt{x+1} = (x+1)^{1/2}\): \[= 3\ln(x+1) - \ln x - \tfrac{1}{2}\ln(x+1)\]
  8. c) Collect\(3 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{2}\): \[= \tfrac{5}{2}\ln(x+1) - \ln x\]

Answerlog 5 + 3 log x;   ln(x+5) − 3 ln x;   (5/2) ln(x+1) − ln x

Part (b) was the poll question.

log(5x³) is not 3 log(5x)

The power law lifts out an exponent on the whole input, and here the cube applies only to the \(x\). Split the product first, then bring down the 3 from \(x^3\) alone.

Three things part (c) is testing

That the minus sign in front of the bracket reaches both terms of the denominator, giving \(-\ln x - \frac{1}{2}\ln(x+1)\), not \(-\ln x + \frac{1}{2}\ln(x+1)\).

That a square root is the power \(\frac{1}{2}\), so it comes out as a coefficient like any other exponent.

And that the two \(\ln(x+1)\) terms are like terms and should be combined at the end.

Two more. Expand a) \(\ln\dfrac{(4-x)\sqrt{x}}{(3x+2)^2}\)    b) \(\log_2\dfrac{16}{3^x}\)

Show solution
  1. a) Quotient\[\ln\!\left((4-x)\sqrt{x}\right) - \ln\!\left((3x+2)^2\right)\]
  2. a) Product\[= \ln(4-x) + \ln\sqrt{x} - \ln\!\left((3x+2)^2\right)\]
  3. a) Powers\[= \ln(4-x) + \tfrac{1}{2}\ln x - 2\ln(3x+2)\]
  4. b) Quotient\[\log_2 16 - \log_2\!\left(3^x\right)\]
  5. b) Evaluate\(2^4 = 16\), so \(\log_2 16 = 4\)
  6. b) Power\[= 4 - x\log_2 3\]

Answerln(4 − x) + ½ ln x − 2 ln(3x + 2);   4 − x log₂ 3

In (b) the variable is in the exponent, so the power law brings the whole \(x\) out front as a coefficient. And \(\log_2 16\) is one of the logarithms you can do by hand, so it should be evaluated rather than left standing.

ToolChange of base

The three laws work with any base: break a base-7 logarithm apart and you still have base-7 logarithms. But sometimes you want to change the base, for two reasons.

  • The TI 83/84 only computes the common logarithm \(\log\) and the natural logarithm \(\ln\).
  • In calculus, the natural logarithm works much better than any other.

Because this is a calculus course, we only need the change of base to the natural logarithm.

Worth memorizing

  • \[\log_b m = \frac{\ln m}{\ln b}\]

Express \(\log_2 7\) using natural logarithms, and evaluate it.

Show solution
  1. Apply\[\log_2 7 = \frac{\ln 7}{\ln 2}\]
  2. Compute\[\approx 2.80735\]

Answerln 7 / ln 2 ≈ 2.8074

Sanity check: \(2^2 = 4\) and \(2^3 = 8\), and 7 lies between them, so \(\log_2 7\) must be between 2 and 3.

The same trick lets you graph a logarithm of any base on a calculator. To see \(y = \log_2 x\), enter

\[y = \frac{\ln x}{\ln 2}\]

Reading the graph back

The graph says \(\log_2 x = 5\) at \(x = 32\), and that is right: \(2^5 = 32\). Checking a graph against a fact you can verify by hand is the fastest way to catch a mistyped formula.

The denominator is \(\ln 2\), a constant. Dividing by it is what rescales the natural logarithm into base 2. The shape of the graph remains the same.

Worked exampleSolving equations

Solve \(7 \cdot 5^x = 44\).

Show solution

Two routes get there. The first isolates the exponential; the second applies \(\ln\) to both sides and uses the laws.

  1. Route 1isolate: \[5^x = \frac{44}{7}\]
  2. Switch\[x = \log_5\!\left(\frac{44}{7}\right)\]
  3. Route 2apply \(\ln\) to both sides: \[\ln\!\left(7 \cdot 5^x\right) = \ln 44\]
  4. Product law\[\ln 7 + \ln\!\left(5^x\right) = \ln 44\]
  5. Power law\[\ln 7 + x\ln 5 = \ln 44\]
  6. Solve\[x\ln 5 = \ln 44 - \ln 7 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = \frac{\ln 44 - \ln 7}{\ln 5}\]

Answerx = log₅(44/7) = (ln 44 − ln 7)/ln 5 ≈ 1.1422

The two answers agree, as they must: change of base turns the first into the second, since \(\log_5\frac{44}{7} = \frac{\ln(44/7)}{\ln 5}\) and \(\ln\frac{44}{7} = \ln 44 - \ln 7\). Route 2 is the one that generalizes, so it is worth being comfortable with.

Solve \(10^{\log(8x + 7)} = 6\).

Show solution

Again two routes, and the second is almost instant.

  1. Route 1switch to log form: \[\log(8x+7) = \log 6\]
  2. Comparesame logarithm on both sides, so \[8x + 7 = 6\]
  3. Route 2\(10^{\square}\) and \(\log\square\) are inverse functions, so they cancel outright: \[8x + 7 = 6\]
  4. Solve\[8x = -1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = -\frac{1}{8}\]
  5. Check domain\(8\left(-\frac18\right) + 7 = 6 > 0\), so the logarithm is defined

Answerx = −1/8

The negative answer is fine here. What has to be positive is the input \(8x+7\), which equals 6, not \(x\) itself.

Solve \(1550 \cdot (1.07)^t = 3100\).

Show solution

Notice that 3100 is exactly twice 1550, so this asks how long an investment at 7% takes to double.

  1. Divide\[(1.07)^t = \frac{3100}{1550} = 2\]
  2. Apply ln\[\ln\!\left(1.07^t\right) = \ln 2\]
  3. Power law\[t\ln(1.07) = \ln 2\]
  4. Solve\[t = \frac{\ln 2}{\ln 1.07} \approx 10.245\]

Answert = ln 2 / ln 1.07 ≈ 10.24 years

Check: \(1550(1.07)^{10.2448} = 3100\). ✓

The pattern in all three

Every one of these had the unknown in an exponent, and every one was freed the same way: isolate the exponential, then apply a logarithm and use the power law to bring the exponent down to ground level.
